### The ladder editor (Task 1)

- **O1. What the four presets are.** *Default:* Off (nothing after the first alert); Gentle (a panel after 10 seconds, silent, until acknowledged); On call (a panel after 10 seconds, and then the first alert's sound again every 30 seconds, up to 20 times or 10 minutes: the §14 defaults); Wake me (a panel after 5 seconds, and then the same every 15 seconds with no limit on repeats and no time limit, O2). None includes a Shortcut. What repeats is settled in Ruling 5. *Otherwise:* the numbers change in `EscalationEditing` and its golden test, and nothing else. [Task 1]
- **O2. Wake me's time limit.** *Default:* none. Wake me repeats every 15 seconds with no limit on repeats and no limit on time, until it is acknowledged, which is what "until I acknowledge" says; the panel, the pulsing icon and the menu stay with it. *Cost accepted:* for as long as it is unacknowledged the Mac is held awake and sounding (`EscalationCoordinator.swift:196-198`), for a page already unanswered for an hour, and the repeat every 15 seconds goes on for someone asleep or away; the editor's caption beneath a repeat with no limit says so (Task 1), and Acknowledge All ends it. *Otherwise:* a one-hour limit on time, so a Mac left alone stops sounding after an hour while the panel, the icon and the menu stay; someone away from the Mac, or asleep through that hour, on a rule with no Shortcut, is then not sounded at after it, which is the unsafe answer. "No limit" is one tick away in Customise either way. Only the preset's time limit and its golden test change. [Task 1]
- **O15. A Shortcut the Shortcuts app does not list, and what clears a Shortcut that did not run.** *(a) A name that is not found.* *Default:* a warning, not a refusal. The rule stays in effect whole, tier 4 included, and tier 4 still tries the name when it fires: a wrong name then fails at that moment and says so through the held "Shortcut did not run" line, and until then the menu, the icon, the editor and the on-call check all carry a ⚠︎ naming the Shortcut (Ruling 21). Nothing is silenced by a wrong name, and nothing that would have run is stopped. *Otherwise:* tier 4 is left off while the name is not found, tiers 1 to 3 running, with the same warning (a phone page is then certain not to go until the name is fixed, even when the list was out of date or `shortcuts run` forgives a difference of case, which was not measured); or the whole rule is refused as it is today (`RuleSetCodec.swift:127-134`). Whichever is chosen, a rule that is not in effect for any reason is an alarm while on call (Ruling 9). *(b) What clears a held "Shortcut did not run".* *Default:* only a launch of the same Shortcut, whether a real one or Test Shortcut, which is the owner's own word: a successful Test Shortcut of that name. Nothing else clears it, so a record of a page that did not reach the phone goes only with a success, and it stays in view, as the ⚠︎ line and the slashed bell, until then or until the app quits; the failed row stays in the Inspector as history either way. *Cost accepted:* in the rename-and-fix flow (the Shortcut renamed and the rule updated) a test of the new name cannot clear the old name's failure, so the bell stays slashed until the old name launches or the app quits. *Otherwise:* a reload in which no enabled rule names that Shortcut at tier 4 any more also clears the ⚠︎ line and the slashed bell, and nothing else, which ends the wait after a rename; it is the unsafe answer, since a record of a page that did not reach the phone then goes without a success, on the ground that the path it recorded no longer exists and the rule that replaced it is either warned about (a name not found) or reports its own failure when it fires. [Task 1, Rulings 6 and 21]

### On-call mode (Task 2)

- **O4. On-call mode outlives a relaunch and never expires, and its self-test runs every 5 minutes.** *Default:* on-call state is one saved date, and the menu says "since Mon 09:00", so a forgotten switch is visible. Self-tests run every 5 minutes while on call and every 30 otherwise: 5 minutes is §14's figure, fixed and not configurable, and self-tests continue during a snooze. *Otherwise:* memory only, so a relaunch silently returns the app to off call; an expiry after some hours, which switches it off for someone who is still on call; or a Settings control for the interval, which lets a user hide an outage behind a long one. Each self-test is a real banner, drawn on a shared screen, so the interval is judged live before 1.0 (Task 9). [Task 2, Rulings 7 and 8]
- **O5. What "louder" means, and whether the alarm ever stops.** "Louder" is this plan's reading of the owner's "on-call mode with a faster self-test", and not the owner's words. The faster self-test is the core of it (Ruling 8). *Default:* the faster self-test and five parts beyond it, each of which can be cut on its own: (1) the health alarm's beep repeated on each on-call interval while a fault stands, every 5 minutes for the first six and every 30 minutes after that for as long as the fault stands, so the beep never stops while a fault does, with capture that has stayed unverified for two intervals (10 minutes) counting as a fault; (2) `OnCallWatch`, which opens the check window and sounds one beep whenever a finding the health alarm cannot see appears while on call (a rule not in effect, no rule enabled, a Shortcut name not found, the login item off), including after a relaunch that restores on-call mode and after every reload or save, and which opens it without a beep when the output turns silent or Alert volume reads zero; (3) the check window, and the warning lines under the switch that the menu does not already carry; (4) two icon states, a problem (which an output muted on call, Alert volume at zero on call and capture unverified past the bound join) and on call; (5) the quit prompt (O6). The rule, Shortcut and login findings beep once each time one appears or its count rises, as the health alarm does off call, and do not repeat: the icon and the menu keep showing them. "Muted apps" means apps on the mute checklist that are not yet confirmed, counted and never named, and the Mac's sound output being muted. If you meant apps whose banners are switched off in System Settings, that per-app setting cannot be read (`docs/dev/notes/2026-09-11-m1-findings.md:443-447`), so the first run's mute step says that only the sound is turned off and banners must stay on. A row on the escalation panel for health is not built: it would share a window whose Acknowledge All is a promise about escalations. *Otherwise:* the beep stops after six, leaving the icon and the menu line (a fault that stands all night is then silent after half an hour); or it repeats every 5 minutes without end; the rule, Shortcut and login findings repeating with the health beep; the panel row, with the hotkey excluded from it; any of parts (1) to (5) cut, which Task 2's matching bullet names; or a different meaning of "muted apps", which changes one finding. [Task 2, Rulings 9 and 10]
- **O6. Quitting while on call asks first.** *Default:* yes, as it does while an alert is listed, except when the quit is a log out, a restart or a shut down, which the app never holds up with a prompt of its own (the unsaved-draft sheet still holds one until it is answered) (Ruling 10: it reads the quit's own reason and the system's power-off notice, and a quit it cannot classify is asked about). The prompt is an ordinary alert with capture running behind it (Ruling 22), so a stray ⌘Q costs an answer and not an outage. *Cost accepted:* the power-off notice excuses both prompts for 2 minutes, and the app is told nothing when the user cancels the system's own log-out or shut-down dialog (this SDK names no notification for it, `NSWorkspace.h`, read 2026-09-30), so for up to 2 minutes after such a cancel a menu Quit or ⌘Q skips the prompt and ends on-call alerting without a word. *Otherwise:* it never asks, so quitting ends on-call alerting without a word; or it asks on a log out as well, which can abort one; or a shorter notice lifetime (`QuitPolicy.noticeLifetime`), which narrows that exposure and, where the quit's own reason is not delivered, risks a slow log out meeting the prompt. If the check that gates Task 2's sixth commit finds neither signal on a real log out, the on-call prompt is dropped, which is the first of those, and the escalation prompt stays as it is today. [Task 2]
- **O7. What on-call mode does about a sleeping Mac.** *Default:* hold the Mac awake against idle sleep while on call, with a second `PowerAssertion` of its own so that an escalation ending does not release it (it is not a lid close, it costs battery for as long as on-call mode is on, and the menu says so); a standing line in the menu and the check window, "A Mac that sleeps, with its lid closed or put to sleep by hand, captures nothing, and SignalLadder cannot wake it", never urgent and worded like the Focus line; and, while on call, a self-test on waking, with the ordinary follow-up two minutes later, because the minutes after a wake are when an outage is most likely: capture that has not been verified two minutes after a wake counts as a fault and beeps (Ruling 9). A self-test right after a sleep can fail once on a healthy app (`CaptureHealth.swift:15-17`), so the default accepts at most one "did not complete" beep per wake, cleared by the retry a minute later. The hold is gated: its commit merges only after M4's unticked check that `.userInitiated` keeps a Mac that can sleep awake (`docs/dev/plans/2026-09-25-m4-escalation.md:198`, `Sources/SignalLadder/PowerAssertion.swift:4-8`) has been made, for this hold, on a Mac with default power settings, and recorded (Task 2). *Departure, only if that check fails or is not made:* the hold is not built, and a Mac that idle-sleeps while on call captures nothing, told by the standing line and the self-test on waking and by nothing else, which is the largest blind spot the plan has. *Otherwise:* no hold and no self-test on waking (the health line then reads "Unverified" for up to 5 minutes after a wake, and the alarm's bound after a wake is the ordinary 10 minutes, one constant); or a retrospective line, "Asleep 02:10 to 06:40 — nothing was captured then", which `EscalationCoordinator.checkForSleep` could record (`EscalationCoordinator.swift:302-321`), in memory, kept until on-call is switched off and lost on a relaunch. [Task 2]

### Snooze (Task 4)

- **O8. What a snooze may quiet, and for how long.** *Default:* only rules that say "Stay quiet while I have snoozed", a flag that defaults off and that no preset sets; only when the rule alerts aloud; and never a rule whose last step is a Shortcut, whatever its box says, because the phone page is the alert that reaches someone away from the Mac and a snooze must not be able to swallow it. So a snooze cannot swallow a page unless someone ticked that box on a rule that makes a sound and pages nobody. A ticked rule is held whole, its ladder included, and the editor says so beside the box; a rule that makes no sound is never held, so a silent phone page still goes. The durations are 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 1 hour and 2 hours; 2 hours is the longest, there is no indefinite snooze, and choosing again replaces the end time. A snooze is global: no per-app snooze, no calendar link ("until the meeting ends" would need a new permission), no per-rule end time. It never touches an escalation already running, and the menu offers Acknowledge All beside it. *Otherwise:* a ticked rule whose last step is a Shortcut is held whole, the Shortcut included, which is the owner's opt-in to a snooze that can silence a phone page (the editor then says so beside the box); every rule that alerts aloud without a ladder (no format change, but Gentle then cannot be snoozed in a meeting), or every rule except those opted out (the least safe); or a snooze that also quiets the sound of quieted rules' running ladders, which needs a rule id in the coordinator and a check at every sound tier. [Task 4, Ruling 12]
- **O9. What the held summary shows, how long it lasts, and what tells you it is there.** *Default:* what a snooze held is saved as counts per rule, never as anything a notification said, and kept until the user dismisses it: an ended snooze, a relaunch and a new snooze do not clear it, and a new snooze adds to it. The menu names rules and counts ("6 matches held while snoozed: On-call mentions ×2, Team chatter ×4") as top-level items, with Dismiss beside it. When a snooze runs out having held something, the app makes one sound, once for what was held, and a snooze that held nothing makes none. The status icon shows its own state while a summary exists, ranked above on call, and its description and tooltip, like the snoozed icon's, still say "on call" while the mode is on. *Otherwise:* no sound, only the icon and the menu line; the on-call glyph ranked above the held one; a summary in the menu alone that resets at the next snooze (which could wipe a held page); or a panel row. [Task 4, Ruling 12]
- **O10. Snooze and on-call mode.** *Default:* switching on-call on ends a snooze, and the menu says "Snooze ended — you are on call". Starting a snooze while already on call is allowed, and the Snooze submenu then says so in one line: "You are on call. A snooze quiets every rule you ticked." (Under O14 "in", and only when a ticked rule is one that applies only while on call, the line adds "including any that apply only while you are on call".) *Otherwise:* a snooze cannot be started while on call, or the two stay independent with no line. ### Burst handling (Task 5)

- **O11. What counts as one burst, when a long one is paged again, and whether the Shortcut is told the count.** Three choices, each with its own default. *(a) What joins.* *Default:* a match for a rule joins the escalation already running for it (one panel row, one count, one ladder, one Shortcut page) in two cases. While the escalation's repeating step (tier 3) is still going and its ladder is unchanged, the ladder itself is the burst, with no time window. And for a ladder with no repeating step (Gentle, or a panel with a Shortcut), a match joins when it comes within 60 seconds of the previous match of that escalation, a rolling gap; a match after a longer quiet begins a new escalation. The second case is what "one Shortcut page per burst" needs where nothing repeats, and it forks nothing, since there is no repeat to fork. Joining never silences anything by itself: a joined match plays its own first alert, its spoken line included, unless the next repeat is due within 60 seconds (or one interval, if shorter), is not the last repeat the ladder will make, and something audible from this escalation has been heard without a fault (a previous repeat, or before any, its first alert), in which case the repeat stands in for its alert. That is a trade the owner should see: the repeat is the alert of the escalation's first match (tier 3 runs for it, `EscalationCoordinator.swift:364`, and a preset repeats a sound alone), so a silently joined match's own spoken line is never voiced, and the panel shows only the rule's name and a count, so two different incidents in one burst can sound like one. Silence needs proof that something audible is about to happen, and the absence of a failure is not proof. Rules with no ladder are not coalesced. *Otherwise:* another gap; a joined match that never plays its own alert (quieter, and a second person's page can then wait a whole repeat interval); a joined match that always speaks its own line, where only its sound waits for the repeat (Task 5 then runs the match's spoken line through the same sound closures in the case it would have joined silently, and a rule that speaks is heard once per match in a burst); or no joining, so a storm makes a row and a page for every match. *(b) When a long escalation pages the phone again.* *Default:* a joined match runs the Shortcut again when the last run was at least 10 minutes ago, so a second incident inside one long escalation is paged and a storm is not paged each time; a match that arrives sooner is owed a page, sent when the 10 minutes are up, so the last incident of a burst is never left unpaged; and at once, inside the 10 minutes, when the last run failed, but never while a run is still pending, so a phone page that did not go is retried by the next match and not left for 10 minutes. *Otherwise:* another time; no owed page (an incident that arrives inside the 10 minutes is then paged only if another match follows after them); no exemption for a failed run (a failed page then waits the whole time for another match to retry it); or never (a second incident inside one unacknowledged escalation is then never paged to the phone). *(c) Whether the Shortcut is told how many matches.* *Default:* no. Its input stays the four fields that the documentation and its privacy test name (`Tests/ShortcutRunnerTests/ShortcutRunnerTests.swift:79`), the page cannot say "7 matches", and no page that says "four fields" changes. *Otherwise:* `ShortcutRunner.Fields` gains `matchCount`, a whole number with no content, passed as it stands when a run starts; that test becomes one that names the five keys and no other, and the pages that say "four fields" are corrected (Task 5 lists them). [Task 5, Ruling 14]

### Launch at login (Task 6)

- **O12. ## Global Constraints

- **When two behaviours are possible, take the one that cannot lose or silence a page.** A match joins an escalation so that it is counted and never forks a ladder, is silent only with proof that something audible is about to happen, and is still paged if it joins too soon after a page (Ruling 14); a Shortcut page that failed is tried again by the next match (Ruling 14); a held "Shortcut did not run" goes only with a launch of that Shortcut (Ruling 6); Wake me repeats until it is acknowledged (O2); a snooze never holds a rule whose last step is a Shortcut, and what a snooze held is kept until the user has seen it (Ruling 12); a saved value that cannot be read reads as on call, not as off (Ruling 7); a quit that cannot be classified is asked about, and the system's notice of a log out excuses the question for two minutes and no longer (Ruling 10); an unknown login-item error is shown as a failure; a Shortcut that cannot be found is a warning and the rule stays in effect (Ruling 21); a rule that is not in effect, no rule being enabled, a login item that is off, an output that is muted, Alert volume at zero and a capture that stays unverified are each an alarm while on call (Ruling 9); and where a choice is between one alert too many and one too few, it is one too many. **2. The rules file moves once, to version 5, and never ahead of what acts on it.** `RuleSetCodec.currentVersion` becomes 5 (`RuleSetCodec.swift:23`), in Task 4, for the one key 1.0 adds, `quietWhenSnoozed`. `version(for:)` (`RuleSetCodec.swift:282-286`) returns 5 only when some rule has `quietWhenSnoozed` set (and, if O14 is answered in, when some rule's condition uses `onCall`); otherwise it answers as it does now, with the lowest version that holds the rules, so a rule set that uses neither is written at 1, 2, 3 or 4 whatever version the file declared (a file that declared 4 and holds no escalation is written back as 2 or 3). The check for a file declared below 5 goes first in the chain at `RuleSetCodec.swift:103-109`, newest first, so a version-1 file whose rule speaks, escalates and is quieted by a snooze gets one message, naming 5, and it names which feature needs it. An older build reading a version-5 file refuses the whole file with its "needs a newer SignalLadder" state (`RuleSetCodec.swift:258`), which is the loud failure; without the bump it would refuse one rule with a confusing message about an unknown key. The 0.1.0 release has `currentVersion` 3 (`git show v0.1.0`) and so refuses any file 1.0 writes at 4 or 5 the same way; `docs/rules-format.md` says so, where a downgrade would meet it. Two things were weighed and refused. Version 4 has not been in a tagged release, since `CHANGELOG.md` lists the ladder under Unreleased, but `main` has read and written it since the ladder merged, a build from source can hold such a file, and giving one number a second meaning is what the lowest-version discipline exists to stop. And one version for each feature (5 for `onCall`, 6 for the flag) is what a build that shipped one feature without the other would need; on the default 1.0 ships one key, and with O14 "in" it ships both, so one number is exact either way. With O14 "in" the sharing has a cost, stated and not hidden: between the merges of Tasks 3 and 4, a build of `main` reads version 5 and refuses a rule that carries `quietWhenSnoozed` as one with an unknown key, one rule at a time and by name, which is loud and is not a misread; nothing is tagged in that window, and only a build from source can meet it. `quietWhenSnoozed` is written only when true, so ordinary files do not need version 5. Reading it follows every other optional key: absent, `null` and an explicit `false` all mean the default, and none needs version 5 (`Rule.swift:223-226` reads `enabled`, `alert` and `escalation` the same way, and `decodeIfPresent` returns nil for `null`, as a scratch decode on 2026-09-30 confirmed); a value that is not a Boolean (`"yes"`, `1`) is refused with its location. With O14 "in", the `onCall` condition differs on purpose: its key is what makes a node an on-call node, so `{"onCall": null}` has no meaning and is refused with `"yes"` and `1`. **The editor and the menu agree, for every versioned key.** A rule the loader refuses for its file's version must not look clean in the editor, because that is the silencing direction for a rule that may page someone. `RulesDocument.load` discards the version it reads (`RulesDocument.swift:34`) and `RulesDocument.problems(in:sounds:)` passes none (`RulesDocument.swift:57-59`), so the editor and the loader have disagreed about exactly this. The model now keeps the loaded file's version, `RulesDocument.problems(in:sounds:fileVersion:)` takes it, and `RuleEditorModel` passes it for a rule exactly as it stands in `loadedRules`, which is the rule the loader is still refusing, and nil for one the draft has changed or added, which a save writes at the version it needs. The version travels with the rules it was read with (`RulesDocument.editable` carries it), `RuleEditorModel.show(_:)` (`RuleEditorModel.swift:81-93`) is the only place `loadedRules` is set and sets both, and `reloadFromDisk` (`:66-75`), and with it a save, which reads back what it wrote (`didSave`, `:142-147`), leaves the kept version equal to the version on disk, so a rule unchanged since a save reports no old gate problem against a file that now satisfies it. **A rule the editor reports must be one it lets the user fix.** The Save button is enabled only while the draft differs from the loaded rules (`RuleEditorModel.swift:48`, `RuleEditorView.swift:59-61`), and a rule unchanged since load equals its loaded self, so an unedited rule in a file that declares too little would show its problem and offer no way to write it: toggling a field and back returns to equality, and Put into Effect appears only for a file changed since it was put into effect, and only reloads. So Save is also enabled whenever the lowest version that holds the draft's rules exceeds the version the file declares (`RulesDocument.canSave(draft:loaded:fileVersion:)` and the `fileNeedsRewriting(rules:fileVersion:)` it asks, both pure and tested, since the app target has no tests; Revert stays as it is), and the save bar says what Save will do, in `EditorText`: "rules.json declares version 4 but holds a rule that needs 5, so that rule is not running — Save writes version 5 and puts it into effect". Once the file is saved the kept version is the version written, and Save goes back to following the draft. So a hand-written version-4 file holding `quietWhenSnoozed` (or, under O14, `onCall`) is reported in the editor in the loader's words, can be saved as it stands, and cannot look clean while the menu has the rule off; once the rule is edited or the file saved, the problem goes with its cause. A rule refused for any reason is a rule that will not page, and while on call that is an urgent finding (Ruling 9). The other way to make them agree, for the loader to accept a version-5-only key in a file that declares less, was refused: it would give the number a second meaning, against M4's "a version-3 file means exactly what it means today, forever" (`docs/dev/plans/2026-09-25-m4-escalation.md:23`). The order of the tasks keeps the file behind the behaviour: the flag lands in the same task as the gate that reads it (Task 4), and with O14 "in" On-call mode (Task 2) lands before the condition that depends on it (Task 3).

The consequence is that the four definitions are frozen: they are literals in `EscalationEditing`, not references to `PanelAlert.defaultDelaySeconds` and its siblings, and a golden test pins On call equal to today's §14 defaults, so changing a default later is a conscious act and not a quiet turning of every On call ladder into Custom; changing a preset's numbers after 1.0 does that to every file that used it. A preset owns tiers 2 and 3 only. It never includes a Shortcut: a name exists only in the user's Shortcuts app, a blank one switches the whole rule off, first alert included (`RuleSetCodec.swift:78-82`, `:413-416`), and a wrong one is at best a warning (Ruling 21), so a preset that produced either would break or cloud the rule it was chosen for. And it never touches `quietWhenSnoozed`, because a rule that has been opted in to being silenced must have been opted in on purpose (Ruling 12). Off drops the ladder, and a Shortcut with it, which is why the editor asks once when a Shortcut is there (Ruling 5).

**4. Task 1 supersedes the layout of M4's Task 6 and keeps its core.** "Layout A" is the owner's own name, in the 1.0 scope of 2026-09-30 (Ruling 1), for one arrangement of the editor, and this plan builds that arrangement and no other: a four-way "If I don't acknowledge" choice, Off, Gentle, On call and Wake me, at the top of "Then", with a ladder sentence beneath it and every tier control behind a "Customise…" disclosure. **5. **6. A held "Shortcut did not run" is cleared by a launch of the same Shortcut and by nothing else, unless the owner chooses otherwise (O15b).** Today it is cleared by any Shortcut launching, from any escalation (`CapturePipeline.swift:243-244`), and it does not know the Shortcut's name at all (`CapturePipeline.swift:54-59`). The pipeline already holds the discipline that decides this for sounds: "only a sound actually playing clears it" (`CapturePipeline.swift:80-87`). Applied to Shortcuts it means a launch of B is no evidence about A, and a test in the editor that starts B must not tell the menu that A is fixed, which would claim health nobody demonstrated. So `ShortcutFailure` gains `shortcutName`, and both a real launch (a re-page of Ruling 14 included) and a test clear it only on a name match. Two ways of fixing a failure carry no launch of the old name: the Shortcut is renamed and the rule updated, or the rule is deleted or switched off. On the default neither clears it: the ⚠︎ line and the slashed bell stay until that name launches or the app quits, and in the flow the owner would follow (rename the Shortcut in the rule, press Test Shortcut on the new name) the test cannot clear the old name's failure, which is the price of never letting a record of a page that did not reach the phone go without a success; the owner's words name only a successful Test Shortcut, and the default keeps to them. *If the owner chooses otherwise (O15b),* `setRules` (`CapturePipeline.swift:279-288`) also clears it when no enabled rule in the new set names that Shortcut at tier 4, a pure `ShortcutFailure.isStillNamed(by:)`; what it clears is the standing ⚠︎ line and the slashed bell, and the failed row stays in the Inspector as history either way (`InspectorEntry.swift:232-263`). That path is keyed on the name and not on the rule's id on purpose: a rule with no `id` in the file gets a new one at every load (`Rule.swift:221`), so an id check would clear a real failure on the next Reload Rules and announce a fix nobody made, the very thing `CapturePipeline.swift:85-87` refuses for sounds. Two rules that name the same Shortcut keep it held until both stop, which errs towards showing the warning, and a failing rule that has since been refused for another reason is no longer in the loaded set, so the failure clears, and the rules problem the loader reports takes its place on the menu and the icon, which is as loud. The claim is kept as small as the runner's own: a Shortcut that fails after the one-second grace still counts as launched (`ShortcutRunner.swift:131-135`), so the editor's result says "Started “Page me”", never "worked". A failed test is shown beside the button and never held: it may be a typo in an unsaved draft, and held, it would put a ⚠︎ line and the slashed bell on the menu for something the user was still editing. One failure is held at a time, as now.

**7. On-call state is one saved date, and an unreadable one means on.** The key is `onCallSince`, in seconds since 1970, in `UserDefaults`, written by the app target as `MuteWalkthroughMenu` writes its key (`MuteWalkthroughMenu.swift:15-21`). Key present means on; absent, off. One date cannot disagree with itself where a Boolean and a date could, and it gives the menu an honest "since Mon 09:00". A value that is there but not a finite number reads as on, since unknown: the app fails towards alerting, as it does elsewhere. It never expires (O4). It is restored before capture starts (`AppDelegate.swift:186`), so the first schedule of self-tests is the right one. It is restored if the app is started, and nothing in the app makes it start: a crash does not start it again, and a restart or a log out ends all capture and every alarm, because the app is not running, unless macOS starts it at login, which is off until the user chooses it (O12). So on-call mode warns when the login item is not enabled (Ruling 9), and no sentence in the app or its pages says a saved date keeps anyone covered. `docs/getting-started.md:337` says so of the app today ("a restart or a log out leaves it off until you start it") and is rewritten, not dropped, in Task 6.

**8. The self-test interval is one pure function read everywhere, and forgetting a place is a compile error.** `HealthInputs.selfTestInterval` already exists (`HealthEvaluator.swift:47`) and the evaluator already uses it to decide when evidence is too old (`HealthEvaluator.swift:139-141`), but it has a default (`HealthEvaluator.swift:57`) and the app never passes one: the timer (`AppDelegate.swift:285`), the retry cap (`:307`) and both constructions of `HealthInputs` (`:389-398`, `:433-442`) read the 30-minute constant. Changing only the timer would leave health "verified" for 31 minutes while self-tests ran every 5, which is the failure the freshness rule was written after: on 2026-09-25 capture went blind about 80 seconds after a self-test passed and health read verified until quit (`HealthEvaluator.swift:35-42`). So the default is removed, `HealthEvaluator.selfTestInterval(onCall:)` answers for both states (300 s and 1800 s, §14; a `static let selfTestInterval` beside a `static func selfTestInterval(onCall:)` compiles, checked with `swiftc`), the two constructions become one helper, and the retry delay's doubling and cap move into a pure function beside `SelfTestPlan`. That function, `nextRetryDelay(after:onCall:)`, is exactly today's rule off call, the delay doubled and capped at the interval (`AppDelegate.swift:307`): the delays used are 60, 120, 240, 480, 960 and then 1800. On call they are 60, 120, 240 and then 300. The self-test timers are armed with `tolerance = 0`, as the escalation scheduler's are after the drift it was measured to have (`RunLoopEscalationScheduler.swift:33-35`). Left to the system's coalescing (the documentation lets it add tolerance whatever the property says), a late fire against a 60-second `freshnessGrace` (`HealthEvaluator.swift:77`) could turn "verified" into "Unverified" while on call; one line fixes it, at both intervals, since one helper arms both. **Switching modes leaves a pending retry alone.** A failed self-test promises "It will retry in a minute." (`CaptureHealth.swift:79`), and cancelling the retry with no self-test run in its place would leave a standing degraded state waiting up to 30 minutes under words that say otherwise. Turning on runs a self-test at once, which takes the retry's place, so it cancels the retry; turning off runs none, so it keeps the retry and only the steady interval changes. Two costs are accepted, not hidden. The window in which real traffic can attribute a failure (`capturesSinceLastCanary`) shrinks from 30 minutes to 5, so more failures will read as the ambiguous "self-test alert never seen" than as "own alerts not shown". And the banner appears 12 times an hour on whatever a screen share shows (`docs/privacy.md:185`). A cadence that skips a self-test when traffic was just seen was refused: traffic is only used to attribute a failure and never counts as a pass (`HealthEvaluator.swift:120-128`), and an on-call Mac is quiet at night, when it matters most.

**9. What on-call mode warns about is limited to what the app can read, and none of it is a modal alert.** Two things are called "On call": the editor's preset, a ladder that applies whenever its rule matches, and On-call mode, a switch that changes what the app checks (and, if O14 is answered in, lets a rule apply only while it is on). Both names are the owner's own words, so both stay, and Task 2, which is where the switch first exists, puts a line beside the preset saying it applies whether or not On-call mode is on. A second standing advisory says that a sleeping Mac captures nothing, which needs no reading, since it is what sleep is; it is worded as the Focus line is and is never urgent (O7). Muting is the user's word, so it says "not confirmed", as `MuteWalkthroughText.title` already does, and it counts the apps and does not name them, since a name can be a fragment parsed out of a banner (`MuteWalkthrough.swift:54-59`). The Mac's output being muted is read directly (`OutputState.swift:33`), and because a beep cannot be heard through a muted output, that finding is visual, and is shown in all three places a fault is: the menu, the check window and the icon. While on call it feeds the same visual path as a muted output and sounds nothing: `OnCallCheck` has an urgent finding whenever it is true, whether or not a rule sounds, since the beeps are the app's own channel and not a rule's ("SignalLadder's beeps cannot be heard: Alert volume is at zero. **10. What the app does when a mode is switched, a quit is asked and a health report is made is decided in the core, and only carried out in the app target.** Three decisions would otherwise sit in `AppDelegate`, which has no tests. `OnCallSwitch.effects(turningOn:)` returns the ordered list of what to do and `AppDelegate` performs it (Task 4 adds ending a snooze, and its input, to the same list). `HealthAlarmPlan.decide` is the one function that decides the health beep and the banner, and `OnCallWatch.decide` the one that decides the beep and the window for the findings health cannot see (Ruling 9). `QuitPolicy` decides whether a quit is asked about. The quit needs its own reason. `applicationShouldTerminate` (`AppDelegate.swift:202-213`) is what a log out, a shut down, a restart and an update's restart also call, and a `runModal()` prompt there blocks them until answered while `.terminateCancel` aborts them. On-call state never expires (O4), so the prompt would meet every shutdown. The app therefore reads two signals and passes both to `QuitPolicy`: the quit's reason, `kAEQuitReason`, which developers report as an attribute of the quit Apple event, and whose log-out, restart and shut-down codes compile in this SDK (Task 2 lists the values); and the age of the last `NSWorkspace.willPowerOffNotification`, the system's own notice that a log out, a restart or a shut down was requested (the name exists in this SDK and its raw value is `NSWorkspaceWillPowerOffNotification`, read in a scratch program on 2026-09-30; a delivery to this app was not seen when this was written, and one was seen on 2026-10-02 and 2026-10-05: see the note under Task 9's human checks), measured in awake time from the moment it arrived (`EscalationScheduler.awakeTime()`, `EscalationScheduler.swift:20`). `QuitPolicy` asks for neither the on-call prompt nor the escalation prompt M4 added when the reason is a log out, a restart or a shut down, or when a notice arrived no more than 2 minutes ago (`QuitPolicy.noticeLifetime`, 120 seconds of awake time). **The notice has a lifetime, because nothing else would end it.** A log out that is aborted, by Cancel on the unsaved-draft sheet that stays in place (`AppDelegate.swift:215-219`) or by another app refusing, would otherwise leave a flag set for the rest of the session, and every later menu Quit or ⌘Q would skip both prompts, which takes away a safeguard that exists today and that nothing would show gone. A notice older than 2 minutes is ignored, so such a quit asks again, and the app also forgets the notice whenever a quit is answered with cancel. A cancel made in the system's own dialog reaches the app as nothing (the only power-off name in this SDK's `NSWorkspace.h` is `NSWorkspaceWillPowerOffNotification`, `:320`, read 2026-09-30), so the notice still stands for its 2 minutes then, and O6 states that cost. A prompt that can abort a shutdown is one the system blames on the app, and on-call state is saved and restored. A quit by the user (⌘Q, the menu, the Dock) carries neither signal, and asks; so does one whose code is absent or unknown and that has no notice in the last 2 minutes, which is the safe direction. That either signal is present on a real log out is what developers report and was not seen in this app when this was written (the notice was seen at a real log out on 2026-10-05, and both signals at a real restart on 2026-10-02: see the note under Task 9's human checks); Task 2's sixth commit is gated on that check, made before it merges, on a real log out and a real restart, the app logging the code it read, or none, and whether a notice had arrived and how long before, and nothing else; Task 9 repeats it on the signed build. **If neither arrives**, the on-call prompt is not asked at all, which is O6's "never asks" for on-call mode only, with the escalation prompt left as it is today, because a prompt that can abort a shutdown is the worse failure; the plan changes in the pull request that records it. **Nothing holds the health alarm back.** A hold-back of the beep while the first-run guide is open would let a window left on another Space silence the one alarm that `HealthAlarm.swift:6-16` was written so that no channel shares, for a first-run nicety no listed feature needs; it is out (Ruling 19), and `HealthAlarmPlan` takes no input from the guide.

**11. If O14 is answered in, a rule condition reads the context recorded at capture, and that context has a true `onCall`.** This ruling is written for the option and is not built on the default. §5.5 keeps the engine pure by snapshotting context when a notification is captured. `ContextSnapshot` was built without `onCall` on the standard that "a field that always reads `false` is not a reading" (`ContextSnapshot.swift:7-12`); with a switch behind it, it is one. So `ContextSnapshot.onCall` is required, with no default, and so are the new parameters of `RuleEvaluator.matches(_:_:in:)`, `RuleEngine.firstMatch(for:context:in:)` and `CaptureRingBuffer.record(_:suppressedRepeatCount:onCall:)`: a default would be a placeholder posing as a reading, and a construction site that forgot would then compile. That costs about eighty mechanical test edits, counted by search on 2026-09-30 (36 `record` calls, 22 `matches`, 5 `firstMatch`, 7 `ContextSnapshot` constructions, 5 pipeline constructions and the 3 `insertionIndex` calls in `DryRunTests`), and turns any future forgotten site into a compile error. The live value is `CapturePipeline`'s new `isOnCall` closure, read once per capture, before the row is recorded (`CapturePipeline.swift:189`); the match is evaluated with the row's own recorded context, and so is a preview (`CapturePipeline.swift:283`) and the dry-run (`DryRun.swift:66`), so switching on-call on or off never rewrites what an old row says would have matched. The condition is `{"onCall": true}` or `{"onCall": false}`, mirroring the spec's `onCall(Bool)`; a `context` key that anticipates screen lock is out of 1.0. Adding a case to `RuleCondition` breaks every exhaustive switch over it by design, in seven files (Task 3 lists them), and would silently pass one that compiles: the `default:` arm at `Rule.swift:181` would read an `onCall` node as a `field` node and report a missing key, so that arm becomes explicit. A rule whose only conditions are `onCall` is a trap the editor makes easy to reach (`ConditionEditing.removing` and `adding`, `ConditionEditing.swift:44-60`, `:128-134`, let a user add "While on call" to a new rule and delete the blank field row): while on call it matches every notification and, first match winning, hides every rule below it. So `problems(in:)` (`RuleSetCodec.swift:289-370`, which flags only empty groups today) gains a problem for a condition holding no `field` leaf, and the dry-run says the same in a note. It is a problem, so the rule is switched off until fixed and the editor shows why, which is how an empty `and` group that would match everything is already treated (`RuleSetCodec.swift:300`). **What "applies only while on call" means is decided by meaning and not by looking for the key.** A rule that mentions `onCall` can still apply in both states (`or([onCall(true), field])`), or only off call (`onCall(false)`, `not(onCall(true))`), or never (`and([onCall(true), onCall(false)])`), and every line the app writes about it ("N rules apply only while you are on call", "a snooze will silence this rule while you are on call") must be true of the rule it is written about. `RuleCondition.onCallScope` evaluates the condition three-valued, with every `field` leaf unknown and `onCall` fixed to each state in turn, where `and` is false if any part is false, true if all are true and unknown otherwise, `or` is its mirror, and `not` swaps true and false: a state in which the result is definitely false is one the rule cannot apply in. The scope is `.onCallOnly` when it can apply on call and not off call, `.offCallOnly` for the mirror, `.either` when it can apply in both, and `.neither` when in neither. The count lines, the editor's snooze line and the snooze submenu's line use it; a rule that only looks at on call in some other way gets neutral wording ("this rule looks at whether you are on call") or none.

**12. A snooze quiets only what a rule says it may, keeps what it held until the user has seen it, says aloud once when it runs out, and expires by comparing times.** The guard against "cannot quietly swallow a real page" is that a snooze silences nothing by default. A rule gains `quietWhenSnoozed`, off unless set, and a match is held only when its rule has it set, the rule alerts aloud (`Rule.alertsAloud`, `Rule.swift:115-119`), the rule's last step is not a Shortcut, and a snooze is active; the three conditions on the rule are one pure `Rule.snoozeMayHold`. The second condition is what a snooze is for: it quiets sound. A flagged rule that makes none has nothing to quiet, and holding it would swallow a silent panel or a phone page for no gain, so it is not held and not counted. **A rule whose last step is a Shortcut is never held either, whatever its flag says.** The phone page is the alert that reaches someone away from the Mac, and a snooze that swallowed it would be what the owner asked it not to be, so the guard is in the default and not in a caption: the flag is not cleared, it is ignored while tier 4 is a Shortcut, the editor says so beside the box, and the menu counts such a rule among those still alerting. Holding it whole, the Shortcut included, is the owner's opt-in (O8). This is the fail-safe direction: a snooze that fails to quiet something is visible and fixable, and one that swallows a page is neither, which is also why dedupe is built to make "false positives, never omissions" (`CaptureDeduplicator.swift:31-35`). It is per-rule configuration, not the per-rule state §13 excludes: the snooze itself stays global, one end time, and the menu names what it quiets and what it does not ("Snooze quiets 3 of 5 rules", "Still alerting: On-call mentions") over the enabled rules that alert aloud, so "why didn't it fire?" has an answer and a rule that was never going to sound is in neither list. No preset sets the flag (Ruling 3), so a rule with no ladder is not opted in merely for having none. A held rule is held whole, its ladder included, and the editor says so beside the box, so that nobody ticks it believing the rule's panel or its ladder will still appear. The other guards hold whatever the flag says: 2 hours at most, and no indefinite snooze. Expiry is decided at every read by comparing times, and the end is read as the smaller of the saved end and now plus 2 hours each time it is asked, and not only when a snooze is requested or restored: a timer that never fired (a sleep, App Nap) can never leave the app muted, which is M4's Ruling 14 applied to a second thing, and a wall clock stepped backwards cannot stretch a snooze past 2 hours from now. The wall clock alone is not trusted for the length of a short snooze either, so the controller keeps a second deadline in awake time (`EscalationScheduler.awakeTime()`, `EscalationScheduler.swift:20`), set when the snooze starts, and ends it at the earlier of the two: a clock stepped back an hour cannot stretch a 15-minute snooze to 75 minutes, and a sleep, which stops awake time, is governed by the wall end. The awake deadline is not saved (awake time is counted from boot), so after a relaunch only the wall end and the 2-hour cap apply. What is held is kept. Counts kept only in memory and reset at the next snooze would be wrong three ways: matches held at 10:10 of a 10:00 to 10:30 snooze would be wiped by a snooze started at 10:45 without the menu being opened, a relaunch mid-snooze would show a summary that undercounted without saying so, and a page held five minutes into a 2-hour snooze would be visible only to someone who opened the menu. So the end time and the counts are saved: whole numbers keyed by rule id, with the times of the window, and nothing else. A name or an app cannot be saved (`MuteChecklist` stores digests for a reason, `MuteWalkthrough.swift:54-59`); a name is looked up from the current rules by id when a summary is shown, and a count whose id is not among them reads "a rule that cannot be found by its id" (removed, or written without an id and reloaded since). A rule with no `id` in the file gets a new one at every load (`Rule.swift:221`), not only at a relaunch, so after any Reload Rules or editor Save, both of which end in `reloadRules()` (Ruling 9), its held count reads that way, straight after the reload, which is when the user is most likely to look; the count itself is kept and shown, only the name cannot be. A stable id derived for such rules was considered and not built: two of them with the same name would share it, and Ruling 14's join, which fails open on a reload today, would then merge them. A saved record that cannot be read is said to have existed ("some matches were held and their record could not be read"), never dropped in silence. The counts survive the end of the snooze, a relaunch and a new snooze, which adds to them, and go only when the user dismisses the summary. **A snooze that runs out having held something says so aloud, once.** A menu that must be opened to be seen is where a missed page hides (Ruling 17), and a snooze that ends at the end of a meeting is the moment the user looks up. The sound is the health alarm's own channel, `NSSound.beep()`, which needs neither the notification system nor a rule's sound file and follows Alert volume (Ruling 9); it is given once for what was held, only for a snooze that ran out (ending it from the menu means the user is looking at it), and never for one that held nothing. The controller keeps a whole number of matches held and not yet announced beside the counts: it is owed while no snooze is active and that number is above zero, is settled when the timer fires, at the next read that notices the end (so a Mac that slept through the end announces on waking) and at launch if the app was not running when the snooze ended, and is zeroed when it is settled or when the user ends the snooze. Staying silent is the owner's alternative (O9). The menu shows the summary as top-level items, its line and its Dismiss, and not inside the Snooze submenu, because a submenu that must be opened inside a menu is the same hiding place and the harness reads top-level items; the icon has a state for it, ranked above on call and normal, with its description and tooltip still saying "on call" while the mode is on, so the glyph never tells the user less than the mode does. Every held match is also visible on its Inspector row. Per-app snooze is dropped from 1.0: it would need names stored that could not be shown back.

**13. The snooze gate sits after the match, and it touches neither a ladder already running nor the app's own health.** §5.18 and §4's diagram put snooze before the rule engine, so a snoozed notification would be shown in the Inspector as matching no rule, the state the spec itself calls the most confusing (§7.2). Here the gate is in `CapturePipeline.process` between evaluating and alerting (between `CapturePipeline.swift:195` and `:200`), so the row is annotated "Matched X" and its outcome says "Snoozed", the match sets the menu's last-match line, and the mute walkthrough still counts the app (`appsThatAlerted` is set above the gate, and a test says so). A held match never begins a ladder and never joins one. A running ladder is untouched (O8): a snooze prevents alerts that have not started, and one menu click must not be able to end a page already under way. This leaves M4 Ruling 3's named gap open for running escalations, and the plan says so: a snooze is a gesture for what has not yet begun, and "not now" for an escalation already climbing is still Acknowledge. The self-test, the app's own notifications and the health alarm are never gated, so a blind app still beeps in a meeting; previews and dedupe's suppressed repeats never reach the gate. A snooze is confirmed by the menu and the icon, which show the end as a clock time; the spec's transient confirmation panel is dropped, and a ticking countdown would need a sixth timer.

**14. Burst handling is decided by the coordinator and asked before the first alert, and joining is kept apart from staying silent.** `EscalationCoordinator` already owns whether an escalation is live, converted after a sleep, capped or acknowledged, all proven with `ManualScheduler`, so it owns whether a new match joins one. The pipeline asks through a new closure `joinEscalation: (Rule, CapturedNotification) -> EscalationJoin?` after the snooze gate and before tier 1 (`CapturePipeline.swift:200`), because a design that decided after `beginEscalation` (`CapturePipeline.swift:209-211`) would have played tier 1 for every match already. The join is keyed on `Rule.id`, and a rule without an `id` in the file gets a new one on every reload (`Rule.swift:221`), so a reload fails open, as one more ladder, where keying on the name would merge different rules; many existing coordinator tests build rules with the same name and different ids (`EscalationCoordinatorTests.swift:76-82`). *What joins.* A match joins the newest escalation of its rule when its ladder equals the rule's current `escalation` (`Running.ladder` is a frozen copy, so after a different preset is saved mid-burst the next match begins its own escalation and the old one ends at its cap or on Acknowledge), the escalation is `.live`, and either its repeating step is armed (`timers[.repeating]` is present, so tier 3 exists and has not capped) or its ladder has no tier 3 and the match comes within the quiet gap of the escalation's previous match (O11: 60 seconds, measured from the last match, so rolling). An escalation whose tier 3 has capped (`.capped`) is never joined: the next match begins a fresh one, which is what lets a page 40 minutes later begin afresh. Joining is what makes a burst one escalation: one row, one count, one ladder, and never a second repeating ladder, since under these conditions a rule has at most one. A time window on a repeating ladder was considered and refused: it forks a second ladder as soon as a match falls outside it while a long ladder is still repeating, and a Wake me ladder, which never ends by itself, would fork another every window for as long as it was left, M4 Ruling 7's cut-off-fragments problem again. The gap applies only where there is no repeat to fork, and forks nothing audible: a match after it begins its own escalation and sounds its own alert, as a match does today. It is there at all, where the simplest rule would join only while a repeat is armed, because the owner's scope is one Shortcut page per burst and a ladder with a panel and a Shortcut but no repeat has nothing armed, so without the gap every match of a burst would page the phone. *What is heard.* Silence needs proof that something audible is about to happen, and joining is not that proof. `begin` arms only the tiers a ladder has (`EscalationCoordinator.swift:226-235`) and an escalation stays `.live`, which counts as escalating (`:192`), until something writes another status, and only the tier 3 cap (`:369-375`), an acknowledgement and a sleep do, so under a ladder without tier 3 nothing sounds after tier 1's single alert, and a repeat that is minutes away is no help to a second person's page. So a joined match plays its own first alert, its spoken line included, and is silent, its words then not voiced at all (O11a), only when all three of these hold. Tier 3's next repeat is due within the smaller of one interval and 60 seconds. That repeat is not the last the ladder will make, because after the last nothing would sound for this match (the repeat limit or the time limit ends it, `EscalationCoordinator.swift:369-375`). And something audible has been heard from this escalation without a fault, which is positive evidence and not the absence of a failure: a previous repeat that sounded cleanly (`lastRepeat`), or before any repeat, tier 1's own outcome, which the pipeline passes into `begin` (`AppDelegate.swift:52-55` reads it from the row today).
