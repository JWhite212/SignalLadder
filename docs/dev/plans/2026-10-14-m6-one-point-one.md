**Architecture:** Task 1 is a button and a test, built and merged. Task 2 changes no code. It times real Teams alerts with the lines that the system log and the installed app already write, and it decides whether anything more is built. Behind it, Task 3 is an instrument, built only if Task 2 finds a late alert, and then one fix for each way the instrument can read, built only for the reading that comes. The instrument is a trace of what Notification Centre's tree looks like and when. It is held in `NotificationCore` as types that carry numbers, ordinals and fixed names, so that text cannot reach them. The core wraps each node the tracker reads in a recorder that passes every read through unchanged and reduces what came back to tokens and lengths as it passes (`RecordedNode`). A shape is then made from the very reads the tracker judged, and the monitor makes the same Accessibility calls as the app. The core reduces a banner to a shape, compares it with the one before, says why the tracker left it alone and where a scan's time went, decides what a sample reads and when it prints, decides whether the pointer is inside a banner, and reads a whole run into one of six readings (`CaptureTrace`, `RecordedNode`, `ShapeReader`, `SamplePlan`, `PointerHit`, `CaptureReading`, `CaptureTraceText`). `AXBannerWatcher` gains one optional closure that reports its events, scans and reads, and one method that fetches the windows Notification Centre has and hands them to the core's sample plan. Both do nothing in the app. A new command-line program, `signalladder-shapes`, sets them and prints the lines. In the first round nothing in the app target changes (M5 Ruling 10: the app target carries out what the core decided and decides nothing). The fixes, if they are built, change `BannerTracker` and the small plans beside it. Their tests replay recorded shapes through `FakeNode`. The watcher carries them out through the same one-shot read that the panel's second read uses (`AXBannerWatcher.swift:346-348`). No new dependency, no new library target and no new framework.

## What could explain the delay

The house plans have no section like this one. It is here because the tasks pick among its readings.

Reading the code rules some things out. When reads succeed, there is no deliberate wait of 3 s or more on the path from an event to a row. The longest deliberate waits are the 50 ms read after the first event of a burst and the 0.30 s second read (`AXBannerWatcher.swift:34`, `:39`). The dedupe window drops repeats and delays nothing (`CaptureDeduplicator.swift:31-47`). The panel path adds about 0.35 s from the event when its reads succeed, plus the time its two scans take: the first read comes 0.05 s after the event, and a second at least 0.25 s after the first, armed 0.30 s after it, a delay that already holds the 0.25 s (`BannerTracker.swift:125`, `:197-203`; `AXBannerWatcher.swift:34`, `:39`, `:346-348`). When reading the window list fails while a second read is owed, the watcher tries again up to five times, 0.30 s apart (`secondReadRetryLimit`, `AXBannerWatcher.swift:47`; the retry at `:321-324`), and each failed read can take 0.2 s to time out (`:123`). That adds five retries of up to 0.5 s each to the first 0.35 s, about 2.9 s in all, the same order as the 3.77 s. The retry is owed to a panel row only, since only a panel row sets the second read (`BannerTracker.swift:197-203`). On every other path a failed read of the window list ends the scan, schedules nothing and logs nothing (`AXBannerWatcher.swift:325`), and the capture waits for the next watched event, which can be a layout change 1.6 s or 2.2 s later (`BannerTracker.swift:12-13`). That is G below. A Focus or Do Not Disturb cannot delay a banner that was drawn, because nothing is drawn under either (`m1-findings.md:302-311`). App Nap was not seen to delay a 30-second repeating timer with zero tolerance (`m1-findings.md:723-756`). The watcher's waits are `asyncAfter` blocks with default leeway (`AXBannerWatcher.swift:257`, `:295`), which that spike did not measure. The trace's gap from event to scan measures them. The 2.5 s that the notes give is when sound would begin, "audible after", which is the time to the first buffer plus the silence at the start of the audio, for `AVSpeechSynthesizer.write` in a new process after a long quiet spell (`m1-findings.md:669-672`, `:679-692`). That note does not show whether making the synthesizer blocks the main thread. `AlertPlayer` is `@MainActor` (`AlertPlayer.swift:37`) and makes its synthesizer on first use (`:605-610`), so the question is left to the pipeline figures of Task 3's last commit.

## Owner decisions

These are the decisions that are the owner's: a matter of taste, or of how much risk to accept. There are seven. Each has a recommended default, and every task below is written on the defaults, so the plan can be built as it stands. Where the owner chooses otherwise, the task named in brackets changes as said and nothing else does. Tasks and rulings cite them as (O3). Every engineering choice is a ruling, with its reason, and is not asked again. A default that is not the one that cannot lose or silence a page is marked *Departure*, with the risk it accepts and the safe answer beside it. O3 and O7 are those. This plan's decisions are numbered from O1 again, so the 1.0 plan's are cited as "M5 O12" and so on.

- **O1. How late is too late.** No page gives a target for capture latency. The nearest figures are these. The self-test gives a banner 5.0 s to be read back (`CanaryService.swift:56`). The panel is set to appear 10 s after a match (the spec, `:674`). A transient banner stays up about 5 s (`m1-findings.md:895-896`) or about 9 s (`:32`; the two notes disagree and nothing reconciles them), and a persistent alert until it is dismissed. *Default:* an alert is captured within 1 second of its text being readable in Notification Centre's tree, on the monitor's own copy of the watcher, every time in the measured runs. The time is counted from T0, the first read (a sample or a scan) whose shape holds the alert's new text (Task 3), to the capture, on one clock. It is not counted from the sender's clock, because the app cannot see anything before the tree shows it. It is read to the monitor's resolution: T0 is known to within the sample interval, 0.10 s, plus the sample's own duration. The app's own row has whole-second resolution and is stamped after earlier banners have gone through the pipeline (`InspectorView.swift:38-41`; `AXBannerWatcher.swift:335-344`), so it cannot be tested against 1 s, and a pass on the monitor's copy says nothing of the shipped app's pipeline. The app's rule is this: its row's second is no later than the second after the monitor's capture line's second. An earlier second is a pass, since the two readers run in parallel and either can read first. Anything finer needs the pipeline figures of Task 3's last commit. Task 2 has its own, looser bar for its own measure, from Presenting to audio start, and does not use this one. *Cost accepted:* a bound this tight may need a read after the event, which is a few more reads of Notification Centre per burst, and a delay that comes before the tree shows the text is outside it. *Otherwise:* 2 seconds, which needs fewer extra reads and lets a page start up to 2 s late. Or 5 seconds, the self-test's own bound. A sighting like the owner's is then inside the target, 1.1 builds nothing for it, and Task 3 still records it. [Tasks 3 to 7]
- **O2. How much measuring comes before anything is built.** *Default:* Task 2's two arms. The staged arm is 20 scored pairs of real Teams alerts from one private conversation, a few seconds apart or less, sent by a second person or from a second Teams account, since Teams does not alert for a message the signed-in user sends. A pair is scored when both its alerts are. An alert that joined its escalation silently, or began while another played, has no start line to score by. They are spread over at least two days, with 3 controls from a test app. One factor changes at a time from a base condition (Task 2). The passive arm is the owner's ordinary work, read after each Teams burst and at least every two hours, until 30 scored second alerts are timed. When its build is the release candidate it is also the real-use fortnight (O7). *What the numbers can say.* With none late in 5 staged pairs, a per-pair rate above 45% is ruled out at 95% confidence. In 10 pairs the figure is 26%, in 20 pairs 14% and in 30 pairs 10%. A fault that comes one pair in ten shows at least once in 5 pairs 41% of the time, in 10 pairs 65%, in 20 pairs 88% and in 30 pairs 96%. So 10 pairs miss a one-in-ten fault 35% of the time, and O3 then ships without a fix. These figures are for scored pairs, and a pair whose second alert goes unscored is left out of them. The base condition and its six variations hold 4, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3 and 3 pairs. Those cells are too small to separate one factor from another. They exist so that a late alert, if one comes, was seen under varied conditions, and the condition of each late alert is repeated afterwards. *Cost accepted:* the owner's time and a second person's, about two minutes a pair. The alerts are real messages, so they go to a private chat. *Otherwise:* 10 pairs, which is quicker and misses a one-in-ten fault more often. Or 30, which is slower and firmer. [Task 2]
- **O3. If the delay does not come back.** The sighting was on macOS 26.7.1 and the Mac is on 27.2. A delay seen once can fail to come again. *Departure.* *Default:* if Task 2 finds no late alert, the instrument is not built and no capture change is built on code reading alone. Tasks 3 to 6 are not built, and nor is the monitor. 1.1 ships with Task 1 and the record, and the pages say the delay was seen once, on macOS 26.7.1, was not seen again in the timed alerts on macOS 27.2, and what to send if it is seen again (the times-only lines of Task 2). The same holds if Task 2 finds a late alert, Task 3 is built, and no late alert comes under the monitor in the pairs Task 3 sizes. The note then records that it was seen without the monitor and not with it, nothing more is built, O5 decides whether the monitor stays, and Task 5's bounded re-reads (below) are put to the owner as a choice and not as a default. *Risk accepted:* if the delay is real and rare, or belongs to macOS 26.7.1, 1.1 still has it, and every tier of a ladder starts that much late, because the ladder's clock starts at the match (`EscalationCoordinator.swift:402`). *Safe answer:* build Task 5's bounded re-reads anyway. A read that errs towards capturing cannot lose a page. The cost is a change to the capture module that no measurement asked for, behind Task 5's live checks. Tasks 3 and 4 are not built on this path, so Task 5 builds none of what they hold: no fixture schema, no `ShapeFixture`, no `ShapeReplay` and no `ScriptedNode`. Its tests build their reads from `FakeNode` trees that share an id across scans, as `BannerTrackerTests` does (`:133-143`, `:214-231`), and are named as hand-written, since no shape was recorded. That is the one exception to Ruling 9. [Tasks 2 to 5, and 7]
- **O4. A change that raises no event.** Only if Task 3 finds text arriving with no event the app can watch, at the application level and at the level of the element, and finds no other notification that is raised for it. *Default:* a bounded poll. After the last event, the banner windows are read once a second for at most 10 seconds, and only while a persistent alert or stack is on screen. It ends by itself. *Cost accepted:* the app's only polling so far is the health self-test (`docs/architecture.md:104-110`), and the page that lists every timer says "there are no others" (`:112`). It changes. It also costs at most ten extra reads per burst. *Otherwise:* no poll, and the gap goes on the architecture page as a known gap, so that a change of text with no event waits for the next event. Or a poll for as long as any alert is on screen, which misses nothing and costs idle time for as long as a persistent alert sits there. If a notification the app can watch is raised for the change, it is registered instead and none of this applies. [Task 5]
- **O5. What stays of the measurement.** Only if Task 3 was built. *Default:* the trace types, the recorder, the sample plan, the watcher's closure and method, `signalladder-shapes` and the shape fixtures stay in the repository as the way to look at a capture that arrives late. The architecture page's section on the probe gets the monitor beside it. *Cost accepted:* one more executable target, built with warnings as errors in CI, which runs only when someone asks. *Otherwise:* delete the program and the watcher's closure once the measurement is closed. The core's types and the fixtures stay, because the tests need them. The next late capture then needs a monitor built again. Or keep the monitor as a single file under `docs/dev/spikes`, outside the package, where CI does not build it and it can go stale. [Task 3]

## Global Constraints

- **When two behaviours are possible, take the one that cannot lose or silence a page.** A re-read errs towards capturing. A read that is owed ends. A capture made from a read whose text is partly stale takes its fields from the description and not from the stale children (Ruling 7). Where the choice is between one capture too many and one too few, it is one too many.
- **Notification text never reaches a log, a file, a fixture, a screenshot or the trace.** The pull request template asks every contributor to confirm it. Nothing in this plan takes a screenshot of a Teams alert or saves a line of the system log. What Task 2 and Task 3 commit is an offset, a gap and a fixed label, and never a time of day. What they keep outside the repository, for a day at most, is times and fixed labels.
- **Nothing in this plan reads `rules.json` or the app's saved preferences.** Where a fact lives in either, the plan asks the owner.
- **Tests never make a sound, show a window, run an external process, read or write the real `UserDefaults`, or wait on a real clock.** Time is the `now` that `BannerTracker.scan(_:at:)` takes. The trace's clock is passed in.
- **`NotificationCore` stays pure.** `PurityTests` forbid its importing the capture frameworks and forbid its touching files or the network (`PurityTests.swift:6-30`, `:59-78`). The new core files import Foundation only. They sit directly in `Sources/NotificationCore`, since `PurityTests` read that folder without its subfolders (`PurityTests.swift:15-17`) and would not see a file below it.
- **No network connection, and no new dependency.** The test that holds this is Task 1's, and a new target is under `Sources`, so it holds it too.
- **The app target has no tests, and CI builds with warnings as errors on both toolchains.** Views stay thin. In the first round nothing in the app target changes.
- macOS 14.0 floor, Apple silicon only, non-sandboxed, Developer ID and notarised, no `.xcodeproj`, British spelling, GPL-3.0. All unchanged.

## Rulings

**1. This plan is the 1.1 milestone. It takes the number M6 because it is the next.** The owner decided on 2026-10-14 what 1.1 holds. In: the Settings button that opens the latest release (with the test that makes the no-network promise test-enforced), and finding why a real Teams alert was captured about 3.8 s after it appeared, and fixing it if it can be fixed. Out: checks on macOS 14 and 15, conditions on time, screen lock and how often an app sends, and the Pro edition. Updates stay as the owner decided on the same day: the no-network promise is fixed, option 2 comes first, and options 3 and 4 are not planned (`docs/dev/plans/2026-10-14-updates-options.md`, "Decided").

**2. Time first, measure second, and build only the fix a reading picks.** Task 2 times real alerts with what is installed and decides whether anything more is built. If it finds a late alert, Task 3 builds the instrument and the owner runs it. Its reading is one of six (Ruling 4), and each of Tasks 4 to 6 starts with the reading that builds it: *description ahead* builds Task 4, *no event after readable* or *scan dropped* builds Task 5, and *scan held up* builds Task 6. Task 7 is documents only. It is built when the trace reads *timely* and the owner concludes from the Presenting lines that the delay came before the tree showed the text (D), when the figure was a mismeasure, when the owner's answers say a menu, a dialog or Notification Centre's list was open (E, F), when no late alert comes under the monitor, or when Task 2 finds nothing and O3 stands. A fix is not written before a recorded shape exists to test it against (Ruling 9). For *scan dropped* the evidence is the trace's line of the failed read and not a shape. Two readings can both be true, and then both fixes are built, in the order of the tasks. A task whose reading did not come is not built, and the plan is amended to say so, with the date. Without a reading at all, O3 decides.

**3. The trace holds numbers and fixed names, never text, and the types see to it.** Its record types have no `String`, `Substring`, `Character`, `Data` or `Any` among their stored properties or case payloads. A banner is reduced to counts and to these, and nothing else:
- **Ordinals** for elements and windows, numbered in the order met in one run, never a hash or the system's identity, so that nothing can be guessed back from them. The recorder's table that numbers elements is keyed by the node's identity, an `AnyHashable`, and stays in the recorder. No record holds it.
- **Tokens** for strings, numbered the same way. Two strings that are equal share a token, and two that differ do not. A token says "this is the same string as that one" and nothing more. The table that numbers them is keyed by `Int`, a `Hasher` value, and never by the string. Two strings whose 64-bit hashes collided would share a token, a risk the tracker already runs.
- **Booleans**, and **names** from a fixed list: the subrole (the five the allowlist holds, or "other", never the name of an unlisted one), the notification, a verdict, a phase and a presentation word (`stacked`).
- **Times**: milliseconds on the system's uptime clock, which two programs share.
- **Lengths** in characters and the **time of day** to the millisecond with no date, in the owner's own trace only. The owner keeps that trace in the account's temporary folder, `$TMPDIR`, under `umask 077`, outside the repository and any synced or backed-up folder, to line it up with the Inspector and the system log. It is deleted when its readings and fixtures are cut, and within a day of the run in any case. Neither goes into a committed fixture (Ruling 9), and the note carries offsets and gaps and no time of day.
- Never the app's name, the pid, a title, a body, a description, a value, or the name of anything on the screen. The probe's `app` line is not safe, and the monitor does not have one (`Sources/signalladder-probe/main.swift:51`; `docs/privacy.md:125`).

The recorder reduces each string as it passes and keeps no `String`. Two source-scan tests and three walks with `Mirror` hold the rule, in the way `PurityTests` and `ViewLiteralsTests` hold theirs (Task 3, commits 1 and 4). The scans match whole words, so `AnyHashable` is not `Any`. The walks cover the recorder's own tables and counters, the events and shapes a scan produced, and a sample of every record. They never descend into the node a recorder wraps.

**4. The core decides, the capture module reads, and the program prints (M5 Ruling 10).** Every decision is pure code in `NotificationCore`, tested with `FakeNode` and a scripted node. That means: reducing a banner to a shape; the comparisons with the shape before it (children the same, description fields gained and lost, only a presentation word changed); why the tracker left a banner alone; what a sample reads, what it prints and when a tick is skipped (`SamplePlan`); whether the pointer is inside a banner (`PointerHit`); the line that is printed; and the reading. `AXBannerWatcher` reads the tree and reports. It gains one optional closure and one method, which fetches the window list and hands the windows to the core, and it decides nothing. `AXElementNode` reads the numbers it is asked for and decides nothing. `signalladder-shapes` sets the closure, calls the method on a timer and writes the lines. The six readings are: *timely* (the capture came within O1's target of T0), *description ahead* (A), *no event after readable* (B), *scan held up* (C), *scan dropped* (G) and *unclear*. Task 3 defines each from three instants and the first chance. A reading says "fits", never "is". D, E, F and H are not readings the trace can make alone. D needs the Presenting lines, which the owner sets against the trace's times of day in a private file, or the sender's clock. E and F need the owner's answers. H needs the app's own row set against the monitor's capture. The owner makes them, in the note.

**5. Every word the monitor prints is a constant in `NotificationCore`, and so is every word of a log line the app writes for it.** This extends M5 Ruling 18 (`docs/dev/plans/2026-09-30-m5-one-point-oh.md:118`), which is about windows, menus and labels in views, to a command-line program and to a log line. `CaptureTraceText` holds the reading's sentences, the line that says Accessibility is missing, the line terminator, the choice of output stream and the words of the one log line of Task 3's last commit, in short fixed words. A test reads `Sources/signalladder-shapes` and refuses every string literal in it but the empty one. The program calls one function with a stream argument, and the core supplies the terminator, so that no newline is typed in the program. The precedent is `QuitPolicy.logLine`, where the core builds the whole line from numbers and the app logs what it returns (`QuitPolicy.swift:311`; `AppDelegate.swift:430`; tested at `QuitPolicyTests.swift:620-624`). In the app target, the closure of the last commit is given numbers, calls one core function and logs the line it returns. It decides nothing, and it sits in a file of its own that the same test reads, which may hold the logger's subsystem and category and no other string literal. `logSpeechLatency` is not the precedent, because it keeps its words in `AlertAudio` (`AlertPlayer.swift:772`). There is one exception. Task 5's B2 logs a refusal as the watcher logs its two others, in words typed in the watcher (`AXBannerWatcher.swift:147`, `:156`). It is outside this ruling, which covers the monitor and the one log line of the last commit, and the watcher's log function stays held by convention and review, as the privacy page says.

**6. The watcher changes nothing when no trace is set, and sampling never feeds the tracker.** With the closure nil, the watcher reads, schedules and hands on exactly as it does today. The extra notifications the monitor registers are reported and never start a scan, so the monitor does not change when the watcher reads. Sampling reads every window and reports shapes, but reads no text from a row that was in Notification Centre's list when the list opened, as the tracker reads none (`BannerTracker.swift:149-163`, `:176`; `docs/privacy.md:31`). It never passes what it read to the tracker, so it cannot change what is captured. It runs on the main queue, as the scans do, so it can hold a scan up. It reports its own duration, skips a tick when the last sample ran longer than the interval and reports the skipped ticks, and each scan record carries the time the scan waited behind samples, so that the time it adds can be seen and taken off (Task 3). The watcher has no test target (`docs/architecture.md:827-829`), so this is held by a live check (Task 3, commit 3).

**7. A capture made while the description is ahead of its children takes its fields from the description, less its presentation words.** `NotificationFieldExtractor` takes title, subtitle and body from the text children whenever there are any (`NotificationFieldExtractor.swift:23-26`). If the children are stale, they are the previous alert's, and a row made from them would carry the old alert's title and body under the new alert's app name, and a rule would match the wrong text. So a read in that state is not captured at once, and one that is captured at the deadline hands on no children, so that the extractor reads the description. That parse is lossy in two ways. A comma inside a field splits it, which is the lesser error. And a stack's description ends with ", stacked" (`BannerTracker.swift:28-31`), which the fallback takes for a field: "Teams, A, B, stacked" becomes title A, subtitle B and body "stacked" (`NotificationFieldExtractor.swift:53-66`). A rule on the body would not match, and the page would be silenced. So the core strips the presentation words from the description before it is handed on at the deadline, and a test shows that a deadline capture of a stacked description gives the same title and body as the children would. A read in which only some children have moved is not stale in this sense. Ruling 13 says what happens to it.

**8. A late read errs towards capturing, and every owed read ends.** An owed re-read follows the pattern of the panel's second read: a one-shot block on the main queue, `AXBannerWatcher.secondReadDelay` (0.30 s, `AXBannerWatcher.swift:39`) after the last read, bounded by a count and by a deadline that the tracker keeps against the injected `now`. None is a repeating timer, and none starts without an event or an owed read behind it. A failed read of the window list owes one as well, bounded the same way (Task 5, B3), and `FollowUpPlan` decides it. That is why Task 5's B1 is not polling, and O4's poll is. B1's reads start from an event and end by themselves within a few reads. O4's poll is a read every second with no event behind each. B1's reads are listed in the architecture page's paragraph on other delays (`:116`) whatever O4 says. Nothing in this plan polls without O4's yes, and the principle of `docs/architecture.md:104` stands: the app does nothing until something happens.

**9. Recorded shapes are fixtures, in a folder of their own, and a fix has one before it is written.** `Fixtures/captures/README.md` holds one description string per file, with a note that no corpus has been recorded. Shapes need more than that, and a string field is what must never be in them. So they go in `Fixtures/shapes/`, as the monitor's own lines cut to the seconds around one capture by a core function (`ShapeFixture`), under a schema whose only strings are the fixed names of Ruling 3. The cut keeps tokens, structure, Booleans and millisecond offsets from the first line kept. Tokens and ordinals are renumbered from 1 in order of first appearance within the cut, so that a fixture does not say how many strings or elements the monitor met before it, and the schema test fails on any fixture whose numbering is not dense from 1. It drops lengths and the time of day, because nothing in the tracker uses a length (`BannerTracker.swift:236-262` works on hashes and sets) and a public file need not carry the timing of a real chat. The schema test refuses a length field, a time of day and any other string. The owner reads each fixture before it is committed and writes that into `Fixtures/shapes/README.md`, since a fixture is published for good. A test helper rebuilds a `FakeNode` tree from a fixture. It makes each text from a letter-only word chosen for its token, so that equal tokens give equal strings and different tokens different ones, and each presentation word as the word. A child flagged as a time becomes a fixed time string such as "5m ago", which `NotificationCentreHistory.isRelativeTime` reads as a time (`:72-79`). The tracker then sees the same equalities, subsets and time labels it saw live, and no word anyone wrote. A fixture replays what the tree held at each read. Each scan record carries the ordinals of the remembered elements it read as gone, as numbers, and the replay marks those nodes gone (`FakeNode.isGone`), so that the tracker forgets them as it did live (`BannerTracker.swift:140-146`). Two kinds of test have no shape to use. For *scan dropped* the evidence is the trace's failed-read line, with its retry number of 0, and the test is a table in `FollowUpPlanTests`. And some tests have no recorded input at all: O3's safe answer, the mixed read of Ruling 13, Task 5's stack of seven and its long run, and Task 6's, which need a fake clock. They are written by hand, named as hand-written in their own comments and in the pull request, and read by the owner. That is the one exception to this ruling.

**10. The target is measured from the text being readable, on the monitor's clock, and read at the monitor's resolution.** The row's time is the instant the app read the banner, to the second. It cannot be compared with a sender's clock, so O1's figure is the gap between two instants in one trace, T0 and the capture. T0 is known to within the sample interval, 0.10 s, plus a sample's duration, and only while the sampler is keeping up: a capture whose T0 is its own scan's read is timely only if the sample before it is recent (Task 3), and a skipped tick is reported. The Inspector's second, the Presenting lines and the sender's clock are the owner's cross-checks, in the note (Task 3). A sender's clock is read by a person to about a second or two, so it can call D only when the gap is over about 3 s. The Presenting line is the better reference. The monitor times its own copy of the watcher, so a pass on it says nothing of the shipped app's pipeline. The app's rule is O1's: its row's second is no later than the second after the monitor's capture second, and earlier is a pass.

**11. A result belongs to the build and the macOS it was seen on.** Every run in the note records the macOS build, Teams' version, the version line from Settings and, for a build from source, its commit. For a run with the monitor it also records the monitor's own commit, and the conditions of Task 2. Task 2's passive figures name the build each was timed on, the installed 1.0.0 or a build of `main` at 0b378c0, since O7 lets one build serve both. A result on macOS 27.2 does not carry to 26.7.1 or to 14 and 15, and a run that does not reproduce on 27.2 does not refute the sighting on 26.7.1 (O3, O7).

**12. No fix names Teams.** Capture decides by the tree and not by the app. The allowlist is by subrole (`BannerSubrole.swift:16-22`), and the app's name is only the description's first field (`NotificationFieldExtractor.swift:21`). A fix is shaped by the tree, and a test app's persistent stack must still behave as it does now. Teams is the case that showed the fault, not the rule.

**13. A stack's description is told from a new alert by what it replaces, not by a word.** Today an expand or a collapse is ignored whatever the description says, because equal non-empty children return "not new" (`BannerTracker.swift:253`). That is true in any language, and Task 4 keeps it so. A read is *in flux* when its children equal the last capture's and are not empty, and its description's fields replace the last capture's: it gained at least one field and lost at least one, as sets. A description that only gains fields or only loses them is presentation, whatever the field is. An expand loses ", stacked" and a collapse gains it. A macOS that names the state in another language, or adds a second trailing field, does the same. `PresentationWords` stays as a refinement. Its words are left out of both sets before the test, and they are stripped from a description that is handed on (Ruling 7). One word replacing another, which nothing has shown, reads as flux unless both words are listed. This keeps Ruling 12: the tree decides, and no list of words does. A mixed read, in which some children have moved and the rest have not (a new title over the old body), is new at once, as it is today, because its children are not a subset of the last capture's (`BannerTracker.swift:250`). It is not in flux, and it is captured with the children it holds. It cannot be told from a follow-up from the same sender, whose title is the same and whose body is new, and deferring it would delay every such follow-up for the sake of a case nothing has shown. The cost is the one Ruling 8 accepts: if the rest of the text arrives, the next read is new again and is captured once more, with the whole text. That is one capture too many, which this plan prefers to one too few. A hand-written read pins it (Task 4).

**What is built.**
- **The recorder.** `RecordedNode` wraps an `AccessibilityNode`. It passes every read through unchanged, wraps the children it returns, and forwards the element's identity. As each answer passes, it reduces it: a string becomes a token and a length, trimmed as `BannerTextReader` trims it (`BannerTextReader.swift:18`) so that a token stands for the string the tracker compared, and a description is split into fields as `NotificationCentreHistory.fields(of:)` splits it (`:65-70`). It keeps no `String`. It times each call against an injected clock and counts the reads, and the ones that took 150 ms or more, in the phase the tracker has marked. Its tables (the tokens, and the ordinals keyed by the node's identity) and its counters live in a small value type of their own, so that a walk can cover them without descending into the node it wraps. A shape is built from what was recorded in the pass the tracker judged. There is no second read, so the scan's own timing is not disturbed, a description that moves before the children is seen as the tracker saw it, and the monitor's tracker makes the same Accessibility calls as the app's.
- **The trace.** One type, `CaptureTrace.Event`, with seven kinds:
  - a **notification**, by name, with the number of windows Notification Centre had;
  - a **failed read**, when the window list could not be read, with the retry's number out of five (`AXBannerWatcher.swift:321-324`), or 0 when no read was owed, which today is every failed read but a panel row's second read (`:325`);
  - a **scan**, with its **trigger** (the notification that started it, by name, or an owed re-read) and its start in milliseconds since the first event of the burst. For each of its three phases it carries the milliseconds taken, the reads made and the reads that took 150 ms or more. The phases are the **sweep** (`BannerTracker.swift:140-146`), the **locating** (`:148`) and the **reading** (the panel test and the banners, `:149-209`). It also carries the milliseconds it waited behind samples between its trigger and its start, the end of the latest sample completed before it (as milliseconds before its start) and whether that sample held a token the element's last capture did not, and the ordinals of the remembered elements it read as gone;
  - a **sample**, with the windows met, the banners met, its duration and the ticks skipped since the last, printed when it found something different from the sample before it, and otherwise once every 10 seconds as a heartbeat, so that a working day's output stays small and a quiet stretch can be told from a dead monitor;
  - a **shape**, one banner element at one read, from a scan or from a sample;
  - a **verdict**, from the tracker, for each banner at each scan;
  - a **capture**, when the watcher handed a banner on.
- **A shape** has these parts, and no others:
  - its element's ordinal and its subrole name;
  - its text children, each as a token, a length and whether it reads as a time (`NotificationCentreHistory.isRelativeTime`);
  - its description's fields, each as a token and a length, or as a presentation word;
  - the number of nodes inside it that the locator met, to its depth and node bounds. A hover adds Close and Show inside the alert (`m1-findings.md:1021-1022`), so it raises this count while the text children stay. The note does not say those buttons are direct children, so the count does not rely on it;
  - whether the pointer is inside it, and whether its window is Notification Centre's list (`NotificationCentreHistory.isPanel`, `:51-61`);
  - against the element's previous shape, whether the children are the same, which description fields were gained and which were lost (presentation words counted apart), and whether only a presentation word changed.

  A row that was in Notification Centre's list when the list opened has no shape. It is counted in its window's record and none of its text is read (the sample plan, below).
- **The pointer.** `AXElementNode` reads the element's position and size, after a scan has ended so that the extra reads do not add to the scan, and the watcher reads the mouse (`NSEvent.mouseLocation`) and the height of the primary screen (`NSScreen.screens[0]`). It returns the shapes, the count of windows with no banner, and whether to print: a sample that found nothing different from the one before is withheld, and a heartbeat comes every 10 seconds. For the list it reports the row count and the Boolean, and reads text only from rows that were not there when it first met the list, as the tracker does (`BannerTracker.swift:149-163`, `:176`; `docs/privacy.md:31`), because Notification Centre's history uses the banner subroles (`NotificationCentreHistory.swift:7-9`) and a day of samples would otherwise hold the length and structure of the whole history. The monitor also registers five more notifications on the application element (value changed, created, title changed, selected children changed, focused window changed) and, as the sampler first meets them, value changed and title changed on each banner and each of its text children, since a change may be delivered to the element and not to the application. `AXElementNode` keeps its element private, so it gains an optional registrar, a closure the watcher passes in and the children it makes inherit, and an internal method that calls it with its own element. `AccessibilityNode` gains the matching request, and `RecordedNode` forwards it. The extras are reported and never start a scan (Ruling 6). `AXBannerWatcher.sample()` fetches the window list and hands the windows to `SamplePlan`. It never touches the tracker.
- **The monitor.** `signalladder-shapes`, an executable target that depends on `NotificationCore` and `NotificationCapture`, like the probe and, like it, outside the app bundle. It sets the closure, calls `sample()` every 100 ms from a timer and writes one line per event to standard output. The line is made by the core. As each capture happens it writes that capture's reading, one fixed sentence, to standard error, so the owner sees it in the terminal while the lines go to a file. When it is stopped with ^C it writes all the readings of the run again. It takes one option, whose word is the core's, that leaves the extras unregistered. It needs Accessibility for the terminal it is run from, as the probe does (`docs/architecture.md:840-849`). It makes no network connection, writes no file of its own, prints no text and reads nothing of the app's preferences or rules. Its watcher lines go to the unified log under the app's subsystem, as the probe's do.
- **The reading.** Three instants decide it, and the first chance.

  | Instant | What it is |
  |---|---|
  | **T0** | The first read, a sample or a scan, whose shape carries a token that the element's previous capture did not hold, in any description field or any text child. |
  | **T1** | The first watched notification, one of the app's own four, at or after T0. |
  | **T2** | The capture: the instant the watcher handed the banner on. |

  The **first chance** is the first scan that read the element at or after T0: the scan that made T0, if a scan did, and otherwise the first scan that started at or after it. Each scan record names its trigger.

  `CaptureReading` takes a run's events and O1's target and gives each capture one of six readings. It judges *timely* first. The lateness is T2 less T0. A capture is timely when the lateness is at or under the target, and at exactly the target it is timely, provided T0 is not the capturing scan's own read. If T0 is the capturing scan's own read, the capture is timely only if the latest sample before that scan did not hold the token and ended no more than the target before the capture. The scan record carries the end of that sample. A sample that is not printed found nothing different from the one before it, and one that ended longer ago than the target proves nothing about the stretch since, because the sampler and the scans share the main queue and a long scan stalls both. Otherwise the reading is *unclear* and not *timely*, because a text that was readable earlier in a window the samples missed would look prompt. For a capture later than the target only, in this order:
  - *Scan dropped* (G): a failed read of the window list, with no retry owed (retry 0), came between T0 and T2, and no scan followed it until a later notification. The capture came from that later scan.
  - If the first chance is the scan that captured: *scan held up* (C) when the gap from T1 to its start, less the time it waited behind samples, or its duration, or its sweep, is at or over the target. If the gap alone is at or over the target and the samples account for it, the reading is *unclear*, since the monitor held the scan up. Otherwise *no event after readable* (B), when T1 is more than the target after T0 or there is no T1 at or before T2.
  - If the first chance did not capture, its verdict says why. *Nothing new*, with the description's fields replaced and the children the same, and the capture coming from a later scan with the children changed, is *description ahead* (A). *Empty*, or *awaiting a second read*, with the capture coming from a later notification or an owed re-read, is *no event after readable* (B).
  - Any other late capture is *unclear*, a first chance with no verdict for the element included.

## Task 4: A description that gets ahead of its text (built only if the reading is *description ahead*)

**Files:** `Sources/NotificationCore/BannerTracker.swift`, `PresentationWords.swift`, `Tests/NotificationCoreTests/BannerTrackerTests.swift`, `BannerTrackerJoinTests.swift` (new), `Fixtures/shapes/` (the recorded join, expand, collapse and hover of Task 3's step 5), and the pages below. The watcher is not changed: the owed read reuses the panel's (`AXBannerWatcher.swift:346-348`).

**What it does.** A read is **in flux** by Ruling 13: its text children equal the element's last capture's and are not empty, and its description's fields replace the last capture's. That is the read `Read.isNew` returns false for at `BannerTracker.swift:253`, less the case of a description that only gains or only loses a field, which stays "nothing new". For a read in flux the tracker captures nothing and does not replace what it remembers. It sets `needsSecondRead`, which the watcher already answers with a read 0.30 s later. If a later read finds the children moved, it is new and is captured once, with the new children. If a read finds the description back to what it was, nothing is owed and nothing is captured. The reads are counted, and the second re-read is the last. A deadline of 0.55 s after the first read in flux, against the injected `now`, backs the count up. The re-reads come 0.30 s apart, so the n-th lands at n × 0.30 s or later. A deadline of n × 0.30 s could be missed by the difference between `Date()` and the dispatch clock, and cost another read 0.30 s later: for two re-reads, a third at 0.9 s, which would put the capture near O1's limit. So the deadline is n × 0.30 s less a margin of 0.05 s, which for two re-reads is the 0.55 s. At the last re-read, or at the deadline if the main queue ran late, the tracker captures from the description alone, less its presentation words, and hands on no children (Ruling 7). The worst case is about 0.7 s after the event, inside O1's 1 s, before a scan's own duration. The count and the deadline scale with O1 by that rule, and it is the rule that is stated and not one figure. If O1 is 2 s, the last re-read is the fifth and the deadline is 5 × 0.30 s less 0.05 s, which is 1.45 s. The worst case is the fifth re-read, about 1.5 s after the first read and about 1.6 s after the event. A deadline of 1.5 s would have the flaw above: a late dispatch would cost a sixth read about 1.8 s after the first read, and the worst case would be about 1.85 s. The state is per element, in memory, and ends with the element.

  - *What a cut-short walk may capture.* The M2 handover records that a budget which runs out partway through a stack's children leaves `childMatches` empty, so the stack itself is appended and several alerts collapse into one (`:53`). A banner found whole before the budget ran out is read as it is. A cut-short read proves nothing, so a window is never skipped for good. A banner could be in any of them.
- **C2. A capture held up by the one before it.** Each banner is stamped after the banners before it have gone through the pipeline (`AXBannerWatcher.swift:335-344`). The tracker's `Scan` carries the scan's `now`, the watcher stamps every banner of a scan with it, and hands each to the pipeline in a turn of the main queue of its own. Order is kept. The Inspector's time and the dedupe window's anchor (`CapturePipeline.swift:204`) are then the scan's start. C2 is the form if the second round shows an earlier capture's work holding the next read (H).
