# Changelog

Notable changes to SignalLadder are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Escalation runs. A rule can carry an escalation ladder beside its alert, written as `"version": 4`: a panel, a capped repeat, and a final alert or a Shortcut, each on its own timer from the match, until someone acknowledges it. See [Escalation](docs/rules-format.md#escalation).
- The rule editor sets up a ladder. Under a rule's first alert, **If I don't acknowledge** is a choice of Off, Gentle, On call or Wake me, with a sentence beneath it that says in words what the ladder does, and **Customise…** holds every control of tiers 2 to 4: each tier's switch and delay, the repeat's alert (with **Test Sound** or **Test Speech**), its interval and its two limits, each with **No limit**, and the last step as an alert or a Shortcut. Wake me repeats every 15 seconds with no limit on repeats or time until you acknowledge it, and the editor says beneath it that this keeps the Mac awake and sounding. The part about the Mac is a request to macOS that has not yet been tested on a Mac that can sleep. Choosing a preset leaves a Shortcut you added alone, and **Off** over a ladder that holds a Shortcut asks before it removes it. The file holds the explicit ladder, as before, and never the name of a preset. Beneath the ladder is one more switch, **Stay quiet while I have snoozed**, which is not a tier and which no preset touches: see Snooze for a meeting below. See [In the rule editor](docs/rules-format.md#in-the-rule-editor). The controls were drawn and driven in a test program outside the app, and have since been opened in the app itself. Their live checks are still to do, and the switch has not been seen in the app.
- **Test Shortcut**, beside a Shortcut's name in the rule editor, runs it for real with a made-up test notification whose four fields say it is a test, and shows _Started “Page me”_ or why it did not start. A failed test is shown beside the button and is never held in the menu.
- The escalation panel: a window over every app and every Space, full-screen apps included, that does not take focus. Over full-screen apps and other Spaces, that was seen in a test program on macOS 26.7, not yet in the app itself. It has one row per escalation, newest first, up to six with a count of the rest, naming the rule, when it started and how far it has climbed, never what the notification said.
- You can acknowledge an escalation from its panel row, from **Acknowledge** or **Acknowledge All (N)** at the top of the menu, or with the hotkey ⌃⌥⌘A, which acknowledges all of them and does nothing when nothing is listed. It stops every tier still to come. The menu's item acts on the escalations it listed when the menu was opened, the hotkey acts on everything listed, including one that began while the menu was open, and a panel row's button acts on its own escalation. Acknowledging the last escalation stops the sound playing only if the latest alert to play was an escalation's, so it never cuts off an ordinary rule's alert. If the hotkey cannot be registered, its status code is logged and the menu and panel still work. On macOS 26.7 it needed no permission prompt.
- The menu says how many alerts are escalating and how many were missed while asleep, and reports a Shortcut that did not run, with the reason. While an alert is escalating, the menu-bar icon alternates between a bell with sound waves and a filled one, and a problem's slashed bell takes precedence. A repeat or final alert that could not sound is reported like a first alert that could not. The menu never shows what a notification said.
- An Inspector row whose rule escalated has a line saying how far the ladder got, whether it was acknowledged, and what its final alert or Shortcut did. It never shows what was spoken.
- An escalation that the Mac slept through for more than 5 minutes ends as missed while asleep when it wakes, instead of resuming hours-stale alarms, and stays on the menu, and on the panel if its panel had appeared, until you acknowledge it. A shorter sleep resumes the ladder. Sleep is measured as the time on the clock less the time the Mac was awake. Apple documents that the awake time does not count sleep, but this has not yet been checked on a Mac that sleeps.
- While any tier is still to fire, SignalLadder asks macOS not to let the Mac sleep on its own, and lets go once none is. It does not keep the display awake. This has not yet been tested on a Mac that can sleep.
- Quitting while an alert is listed, or while you are on call, asks first, because quitting stops every alert still escalating and a Shortcut not yet run will not run, and ends on-call alerting and the faster self-test. The question is not asked at a log out, a restart or a shut down, and it is not asked for 2 minutes after the system says one was requested. A quit that cannot be classified is asked about. If another alert begins while the question is up, choosing **Quit** asks again and names the new count. See [Quitting asks whether to quit anyway](docs/troubleshooting.md#quitting-asks-whether-to-quit-anyway). The question was tested and its pieces tried in test programs, and it has not been seen on a real log out or restart.
- A rule naming a Shortcut that is not in the Shortcuts app is reported when the rules load.
- **On-call mode.** The menu's **On Call** item switches it on, with a tick, and the menu says since when (_On call since Mon 09:00_), so a switch you forgot is there to be seen. The time is saved, so the mode is still on after a relaunch, and it never switches itself off. Switching on runs a self-test at once, with another about two minutes later if it passes, and each is a real banner on whatever your screen shows. Switching off leaves escalations already running alone. It changes nothing a rule can read, and it is not the rule editor's **On call** choice, which the editor now says beside it: _On call applies whenever this rule matches, whether or not On-call mode is on._ See [On-call mode](docs/getting-started.md#on-call-mode). It is built and its decisions are tested, but SignalLadder itself has not been run with it, and its live checks are still to do. They include the status menu and capture while a menu or the quit prompt is up, a real log out and a real restart, the Mac kept awake while you are on call, the **On Call** item and the check window on a real screen, the beeps at the Alert volume, and the icon at menu-bar size.
- A faster self-test while you are on call: every 5 minutes in place of every 30, about 12 banners an hour. The health line reads _Unverified_ once the last success is more than 6 minutes old in place of 31, and a failed self-test is retried after 60, 120 and 240 seconds and then every 5 minutes, where off call the retries go on doubling up to 30 minutes. There is no setting for the interval. Switching on drops a pending retry, since the self-test it runs takes its place, and switching off keeps one.
- The health beep repeats while you are on call. A fault beeps at once, then six more times 5 minutes apart, so seven beeps in the first half hour, and then every 30 minutes for as long as the fault stands. The banner still comes once. Capture that stays unverified for 10 minutes now counts as a fault, and so does capture still unverified 2 minutes after the Mac wakes, and each beeps on the same schedule with no banner. Off call it never sounds, as before. A self-test also runs when the Mac wakes while you are on call, and a healthy app may give one _did not complete_ beep right after a sleep, which the retry a minute later clears. The beeps are the Mac's alert sound, which macOS is reported to play at the Alert volume in System Settings › Sound and not at the output volume. That has not yet been tested.
- **Show On-Call Check…** opens an ordinary window, titled SignalLadder On-Call Check, that lists what SignalLadder can and cannot see: capture not yet verified or the health line's cause, a muted output while a rule sounds, an Alert volume of zero, rules that are not in effect, no rule enabled, rules that only show a panel, Shortcut names that were not found, apps not confirmed muted, and two standing notes, that a Focus hides banners and cannot be read and that a Mac that sleeps captures nothing and cannot be woken. Urgent findings come first, in sentences and counts, never naming a rule, an app or a Shortcut. It opens by itself when switching on finds something urgent and afterwards when something new turns up that the health alarm cannot see, with one beep, and with no beep for a muted output or an Alert volume of zero. **Check Now** runs one self-test and checks everything again. It is never a dialog.
- The menu-bar icon has an on-call state, a bell with a filled badge, described as _SignalLadder — on call_, with _self-test every 5 minutes_ added while self-tests are running. A problem's slashed bell outranks an escalation, which outranks a snooze's moon and the tray for what a snooze held, and those outrank it. While you are on call, capture that has stayed unverified, a muted or zero-volume output while a rule sounds, and an Alert volume of zero also show the slashed bell.
- While you are on call SignalLadder asks macOS not to let the Mac idle-sleep, for as long as the mode is on, with a request of its own that an escalation ending does not release. It costs battery, and the menu says so. A closed lid and a sleep you choose still sleep the Mac, and the display is not held. The request has not yet been tested on a Mac that can sleep.
- The status menu does not change while it is open, so a banner that arrives as you reach for a row cannot move it under your pointer. The icon and the panel update at once, and the menu lists what arrived the next time you open it. The menu's Acknowledge acts only on the escalations it listed when it was opened. One that began while the menu was open stays escalating, with its sound. This was tested and tried on a menu in a test program, and has not been seen in the app itself.
- Capture keeps running while the status menu is open and while the quit prompt is up. It used to pause in both, because its Accessibility source was added to the main run loop in the default mode alone. This was checked with a stand-in for the source in test programs, and has not been seen in the app itself, including that a banner captured then is played and spoken.
- **Launch at login, in a Settings window.** **Settings…** in the menu, after the rules and before **Quit SignalLadder**, and ⌘, in the menu and in the application menu of the main menu, open SignalLadder Settings. It has one switch, **Launch at login**, which is off unless macOS already reports the login item as enabled, as it did, on macOS 26.7.1, for an entry added by hand. SignalLadder asks macOS to list it as a login item only when you press the switch, **Switch on again** or the on-call finding's **Turn on Launch at login**. It does so never at launch, and it never switches the item back on after you switch it off. The sentence under the switch says SignalLadder is set to start when you log in only when macOS reports the login item as enabled, and says otherwise that macOS does not report it so. For an item macOS says is switched off, or is waiting for your approval, the window offers **Open Login Items…** and **Switch on again**. A copy that cannot register, one run from `build/` or from a temporary location macOS gives it, has the switch dimmed, with the reason and _Move SignalLadder to Applications, then open that copy._ When you switched it on and macOS does not report it as enabled, one line in the menu above **Settings…** says _macOS does not report Launch at login as enabled; see Settings_, and the icon does not change. SignalLadder has no guard against a second copy running.
  - Measured on 2026-10-05, on macOS 26.7.1, with a stand-in app signed as SignalLadder is and not with SignalLadder itself: a copy never registered reads as not found, in Applications and outside it; an entry added by hand in System Settings › General › Login Items reads as enabled, so the window shows it as on, and unregistering removed it; and registering twice made one entry. The status is read when SignalLadder starts, when Settings is shown, when you come back to SignalLadder, when the menu opens and at each health check, among other times, and a read took 10 to 21 ms when reads were spread out and 2 to 3 ms back to back. None of this was seen on macOS 14 or 15, so whether an entry added by hand reads as enabled there is not known.
  - Seen on 2026-10-05, on macOS 26.7.1: a Developer ID build run from `build/` opened Settings from **Settings…**, with the switch dimmed and off and the reason shown, and the menu had **Settings…** before **Quit SignalLadder** and no Launch at login line, since it had never been switched on. Not seen: SignalLadder registering itself, macOS then starting SignalLadder at the next log in, a copy in `~/Applications` registering, the window in dark mode, pressing ⌘, in either menu, selecting and copying the version line, a copy macOS runs from a temporary location, errors 3, 6, 11 and 12, and the status of an item switched off in System Settings.
- **A finding about Launch at login while you are on call.** The check lists an urgent finding when macOS does not report Launch at login as enabled: _SignalLadder may not start again after a restart or log out._ and then what was read, _macOS does not report Launch at login as enabled._, or _Launch at login is switched off in System Settings, or is waiting for your approval there._, or, for a copy that cannot register, the reason and the move to Applications. It says "may" and not "will not", because what it has read is only that macOS does not report the item as enabled, and an entry added by hand read as enabled on macOS 26.7.1 alone. The window shows the button the status allows beneath it, **Turn on Launch at login** or **Open Login Items…**, and a line beneath that when a press failed or changed nothing. The menu has one short line, _⚠︎ May not start after a restart or log out — see On-Call Check_, and no button. The window opens and one beep sounds when the finding turns up, once, and it does not sound again while the finding stands. It is checked each time the login item's status is read, which is whenever SignalLadder asks what to tell you: when it starts, after each reload of the rules, at each health check, when you switch **On Call** on, when the menu opens, when you come back to SignalLadder, when Settings is shown, after a press, and when the check window comes to the front or is opened from the menu. So it can turn up at a health check with nobody at the Mac. Off call it says nothing. It has not been seen in the app.
- **Which build is running.** The foot of Settings has one line, in secondary text built to be selected and copied into a bug report, though copying it has not been tried, such as _Version 0.1.0 (1), built from d5937c0 on 2 Oct 2026_. It adds _with local changes_ after the commit for a build made from an edited tree, and says the commit was not recorded for a build with none. `Scripts/make-app.sh` writes three keys into the staged copy of `Info.plist` before it signs, since the signature seals the file: `SLBuildCommit`, `SLBuildDate` and `SLBuildModified`. `SLBuildModified` is true when `git status --porcelain --untracked-files=no` lists anything, so a new file that git does not track yet is not counted, though the build compiles it, and a line without _with local changes_ is not a claim that the build is exactly that commit. Outside a git checkout, in a folder inside another repository, in a checkout with no commit, or when git cannot say, the script writes none of the keys, says why and still builds. `Resources/Info.plist` is unchanged. The script was run on a scratch clone on macOS 26.7.1 and the keys read back, and the window showed the line on 2026-10-05, in a build run from `build/`. Nothing about the line uses the network.
- **Snooze for a meeting.** The menu's **Snooze** item, after the last of the On Call lines and above the capture count, starts a snooze of **15 minutes**, **30 minutes**, **1 hour** or **2 hours**, and **End Snooze** in its submenu ends one. Choosing a length while one runs replaces its end, counted from then. Two hours is the longest, there is no snooze with no end, and the end shows as a clock time (_Snoozed until 15:30 — quiets 3 of 5 rules_). Beside it, while a snooze runs, the menu says which rules it does not quiet (_Still alerting: On-call mentions_), and, whether or not one runs, while no rule could be held, _Snooze quiets no rules yet — tick “Stay quiet while I have snoozed” on a rule that makes a sound and does not run a Shortcut_. The counts are over the enabled rules that make a sound, and the names are your rules' own.
  - **A snooze holds only what you ticked.** A rule gains `"quietWhenSnoozed": true`, which is off unless set and which no preset sets, and the rule editor has a switch for it beneath the ladder, **Stay quiet while I have snoozed**, with a caption that says what it does for the rule on show. A snooze holds a match only when its rule has the box ticked, is switched on, makes a sound or speaks on some tier, and does not end in a Shortcut. A tick on a rule that makes no sound, or whose last step is a Shortcut, is kept and does nothing, and the caption says so, because the page that reaches your phone is the alert that matters most when you are away from the Mac. The self-test, SignalLadder's own banners and the health alarm are never held.
  - **A held match plays nothing and starts no ladder,** and the whole rule is held, its panel and repeats included. It is not hidden: its Inspector row reads _Matched …_ and _Snoozed — no alert_, the menu's last-match line reads _Last match: On-call mentions at 10:10 — held while snoozed_, the mute walkthrough still counts its app, and it neither sets nor clears the line for an alert that could not play. An alert that is already escalating is not touched by a snooze, so its later tiers still fire and acknowledging it still stops it. A held match is counted and shown, and it is not played again when the snooze ends.
  - **What a snooze held is kept until you dismiss it.** The menu names the rules and counts matches, never what a notification said (_6 matches held while snoozed: On-call mentions ×2, Team chatter ×4_), with **Dismiss** beside it, before, during and after a snooze. An ended snooze, a relaunch and a new snooze, which adds to it, do not clear it. **Dismiss** takes out what the line beside it showed, so a match held while the menu was open stays for the next time. A rule with no id of its own gets a new one at each load, so its count then reads _a rule that cannot be found by its id_, and is kept. A saved record that cannot be read is said to have existed: _Some matches were held while snoozed and their record could not be read_.
  - **A snooze that runs out having held something beeps once,** with the Mac's alert sound, so a snooze that ends with the meeting is noticed. A snooze that held nothing makes no sound, and neither does one you end from the menu or one that switching on-call mode on ends. A Mac that slept through the end says so as it wakes, whether or not you are on call, and a relaunch after an end that nobody heard says so once. The beep follows Alert volume, as the other beeps are reported to, and has not yet been heard.
  - **The icon** is a moon while a snooze runs and a tray while something held waits for you and no snooze runs, in the order problem, escalating, snoozed, held, on call, normal. The description and the tooltip name the end or the count (_SignalLadder — snoozed until 15:30, 3 matches held_) and add _and you are on call_ while the mode is on. A snooze never shows the slashed bell, and a problem found during one still does.
  - **On-call mode.** Switching it on ends a snooze, keeps what it held and makes no sound, and the menu says _Snooze ended — you are on call_ for up to 2 hours. You can start a snooze while on call, and its submenu then says _You are on call. A snooze quiets only the rules you ticked that make a sound and do not run a Shortcut._ Switching it off never touches a snooze.
  - **The end is compared with the clock each time it is read,** and not only when a timer fires, so a timer that never fired cannot leave the app quiet. A snooze ends at the earliest of its saved end, 2 hours from now and a deadline in awake time that is not saved, so a clock set back an hour does not stretch a 15-minute snooze, and a sleep is governed by the saved end. After a relaunch only the saved end and the 2 hours apply.
  - **The rules file** moves to `"version": 5` for `quietWhenSnoozed`, written only when true, so a file that does not use it is written at the version it had. A rule with the key in a file that declares less is refused with one message that names the key and 5, and the rule editor says the same, offers Save for a rule you have not changed and writes version 5. A value that is not `true`, `false` or `null` is refused with its location, and `null` and a missing key read as off, as `false` does. A build older than 5 refuses a whole version 5 file. See [Staying quiet while snoozed](docs/rules-format.md#staying-quiet-while-snoozed).
  - It is built and its decisions are tested, but SignalLadder itself has not been run with it. Nothing was seen on screen, nothing real was timed, no beep was heard, no Mac slept through an end and nothing was read from or written to the real preferences. Its live checks are still to do. They include a banner posted while the menu is held open, **Dismiss** pressed with a match held in that time, a real sleep through an end, the beep at the Alert volume, and the moon and the tray at menu-bar size. See [Snooze for a meeting](docs/getting-started.md#snooze-for-a-meeting).
- An app icon: the logo, fitted to Apple's icon grid, in `Resources/AppIcon.icns`. `Scripts/make-icon.sh` regenerates it from `docs/assets/logo.svg`, and a build only copies it into the app.
- `Scripts/release.sh`, which builds, signs, notarises and staples a DMG on the maintainer's Mac, and [a guide to using it](docs/dev/releasing.md). There is still no download. On 2026-09-30, on macOS 26.7.1, it was run for real as a rehearsal, not a release: Apple accepted the app and the DMG and both were stapled. The run on 2026-10-05 was a dry run, which stops short of Apple's notary service.

### Changed
- A rule whose last step names a Shortcut that the Shortcuts app does not list is not refused. It stays in effect, first alert and repeats included, and tier 4 tries the name when it fires. The menu shows a ⚠︎ line under the rules summary with a line for the rule, the icon turns to the slashed bell, and the editor says so beside the name. A name typed by hand can be wrong by a typo or a capital, or a Shortcut can be renamed weeks later, and none of those should switch off a rule that also sounds and repeats. A name is still compared exactly, and a blank name is still refused.
- A Shortcut that did not run is held until that same Shortcut starts again, from an escalation or from **Test Shortcut**. A start of another Shortcut no longer clears it. Reloading the rules clears nothing, so after a name is changed in a rule the ⚠︎ line and the slashed bell stay until the old name starts again or SignalLadder quits.
- `Scripts/verify-live.sh` finds the health line by what it says, so it works while an alert is escalating and its lines head the menu, and it fails and says so when no line says what a health line says. It reads the Inspector's and the rule editor's window titles out of `NotificationCore`, and `HarnessConstantsTests` holds the script to them.
- SignalLadder supports Apple silicon Macs only. It is built for arm64 with no Intel or universal build, and the README, the getting-started guide and the contributing guide now say so.

### Fixed
- The rule editor agrees with the loader about a rule that its file's version is too old for: an alert in a `"version": 1` file, speech in a version 2 file, or a ladder in a version 3 file. The loader refused such a rule and the editor showed it as fine. Now the editor shows the same problem, **Save** is available for it without any edit, and the bar says which version the file declares and which version Save writes. The dry-run used to read as if such a rule were running. For a rule that is switched on, and for a rule below it, it now says _Not in effect until you save_.
- Clicking a stack of persistent alerts to expand it, or collapsing it again, no longer reads the alerts in it a second time. Each click used to add them to the Inspector again, timed to the click, and set off their rules' alerts again, an escalation included. A stack's accessibility description ends with ", stacked", which comes and goes as it expands and collapses while its text stays the same, and that was taken for new text. This was seen on macOS 26.7 with a test app's persistent alerts, not yet with Teams' or Outlook's.

### Security
- A Shortcut run by an escalation is given four fields of the notification (app name, title, subtitle and body) in a temporary file. It is the one place SignalLadder itself writes notification text to disk. It is in a folder of its own that only you can open, is deleted the moment the Shortcut ends, and anything a crash or quit left behind is removed at launch and at quit. What the Shortcut does with the text is up to the Shortcut you wrote.
- A live escalation keeps a copy of its notification in memory until it is finished with, which can be longer than the Inspector's last 50 rows.
- SignalLadder now runs one other program, `/usr/bin/shortcuts`, and only for a rule that names a Shortcut: to list them, to run one as a last step, and to run one when you press **Test Shortcut** in the rule editor. Its output is never logged.
- **Test Shortcut** writes the Shortcut's temporary file too, with a made-up test notification and no notification you received. It really runs the Shortcut, so a Shortcut that sends a message sends it.
- SignalLadder's preferences hold a second key, `onCallSince`: the time on-call mode was switched on, in seconds since 1970. Switching off removes it. It is a date and not notification text, and a value there that cannot be read as a time reads as on-call mode being on.
- SignalLadder's preferences hold a third key, `launchAtLoginWanted`: a Boolean, what you last asked of the Launch at login switch. It is written only when you press the switch or a button that registers, and switching off writes false and does not remove it. A value that is not a Boolean reads as not wanted. It is not notification text.
- SignalLadder's preferences hold a fourth key, `snoozeUntil`: the end of a snooze, in seconds since 1970, as a real number. Ending a snooze, or finding one over, removes it. A value that is in the past, is not finite or is not a number reads as no snooze and is removed, and one further off than 2 hours is cut back to 2 hours and saved as that. It is a date and not notification text.
- SignalLadder's preferences hold a fifth key, `snoozeHeld`: what snoozes held and you have not dismissed, as one dictionary of numbers. `counts` maps a rule's id, as text, to a whole number of matches, `since` is the time of the first hold in seconds since 1970, `unannounced` is a whole number of matches held and not yet announced, and `unreadable` is the whole number 1, written only when an earlier record could not be read. It holds no rule name, no app name and no notification text, because a rule's name is looked up from the current rules when a line is shown. It is written whenever it changes, when a match is held, when what was held is announced, when a snooze that held something is ended and when you dismiss a line, and an empty summary removes the key. A record that cannot be read is said to have existed and never dropped.
- A snooze keeps no notification text anywhere: not in the saved values above, not in the menu's lines or the icon's tooltip, which hold the name of a rule you wrote and counts, and not in the Inspector row it adds, which is the row the match already had.
- SignalLadder imports Apple's ServiceManagement framework, in one file, to register and unregister its own login item. It does so only when you press the switch or a button, and the core stays free of it.
- SignalLadder also reads, with no permission, what macOS reports for its own login item and the folder it is running from, to say whether Launch at login is on and whether it can be offered. A read changes nothing, and only the latest is held, in memory. The status is read when SignalLadder starts, at each health check, when the menu opens, when Settings is shown and when you come back to SignalLadder, among other times.
- SignalLadder reads the Mac's Alert volume, the global preference `com.apple.sound.beep.volume`, as a number, and never writes it. It reads the reason a quit carries and the system's notice that a log out, a restart or a shut down was requested, and keeps only the awake time of the latest notice, in memory.
- The log has a fourth category, `quit`: one line for every quit SignalLadder is asked about, holding the four characters of the quit's reason code, or none, and the age of any power-off notice, or none, at the default level and marked public. It holds nothing a notification said.
- The log has a fifth category, `loginitem`: one line for each request to register or unregister that failed, holding the error's domain and its code, marked public, at the error level. Never the error's description, which can hold a name or a path.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-09-29

The first tagged release. It is source only: there is no signed download yet, so you build it yourself ([getting started](docs/getting-started.md)).

### Added

**Capture**
- Reads notification banners from Notification Centre through the Accessibility API, and splits each into app, title, subtitle and body. It only reads: it never clicks, dismisses or replies.
- A notification that arrives while another banner is still on screen is captured, and each banner is read only once, however many times macOS redraws it. A banner read back with less text than before, because a read timed out, is not mistaken for a new notification.
- A second persistent alert from the same app, which macOS stacks with the first, is captured, and so is every alert after it in the stack. Teams alerts are persistent when Teams is set to Alert in System Settings.
- Opening Notification Centre does not replay the notifications it lists, however recent. SignalLadder recognises the list by how its window is built, checked on macOS 26.7, and a notification that arrives while the list is open is still captured. One that arrives at the very moment the list opens is taken for history, and may be missed.
- Capture recovers when Notification Centre restarts, however often it does.

**Health**
- A self-test: SignalLadder sends itself a silent notification and checks that it can read it back, at launch, two minutes later and then every 30 minutes. The menu shows _Working — verified 3 min ago_, _Unverified — last verified …_ once a self-test is overdue, _Cannot verify itself_ or _NOT capturing notifications_.
- Problems are reported three ways that no single fault can silence together: the menu-bar icon, a beep, and a notification when banners are known to be shown.
- The health line re-checks as soon as whatever blocked a self-test clears, such as Accessibility granted again or SignalLadder's own banners switched back on.
- Do Not Disturb is told apart from a capture failure, so Accessibility is not blamed for a notification that was never drawn.
- The app asks for Accessibility, then for notifications, and picks up a permission granted while it is running without a relaunch.

**The Inspector**
- **Show Inspector…** (⌘I): the last 50 captured notifications, newest first, with each field, the raw text on request, the banner type, how many the same app sent in the last hour, which rule matched and what was done. Held in memory only, and gone when the app quits.
- An empty Inspector says whether nothing has arrived or capture cannot be confirmed, and why.

**Rules**
- Rules in `~/Library/Application Support/com.jamiewhite.signalladder/rules.json`. A rule matches on `app`, `title`, `subtitle`, `body`, `raw` or `subrole` with `equals`, `notEquals`, `contains` or `matches` (a pattern with `*` and `?`), combined with `and`, `or` and `not`. Matching ignores case and accents, and the first enabled rule wins.
- One bad rule never disables the others. Problems, including a sound or voice that is missing, are listed by rule when the rules load, and a damaged file is reported, never overwritten. Unknown keys are errors, so a typo cannot leave a rule quietly silent.
- A rules file is written at the oldest version that can hold it (`1`, `2` with an alert, `3` with speech), so an older build refuses a file it cannot fully read rather than dropping part of it.
- **Reload Rules** (⌘R) previews new rules against everything already captured. The menu shows the rules in effect, the last match, and how many recent notifications the current rules match.
- [`docs/rules-format.md`](docs/rules-format.md), a guide to writing rules by hand.

**The rule editor**
- **Edit Rules…** (⌘E). Rules can be added, duplicated, deleted, switched on and off, and dragged into priority order. A condition builder handles nested _all of_, _any of_ and _not_ groups.
- A live dry-run shows which notifications in memory a rule would match, which rule above it takes others first, and **Move Above** to fix that. It always says whether what you see is in effect yet.
- **Make a Rule from This…** on every Inspector row, and **Add Condition from This Notification**, so a rule starts from a real notification.
- Safe saving: the file is written in one step, the version it replaces is kept as `rules.previous.json`, and a save is refused if the file changed on disk since it was read, with **Reload from Disk**, **Save Anyway** and **Cancel** offered. Save Anyway keeps a dated copy of what it replaces.
- A rules file the editor cannot fully represent opens read-only, with the reason. Closing the editor or quitting with unsaved changes asks whether to save. **Open Rules File in Text Editor…** opens the file itself.

**Alerts**
- A rule's alert can be a sound, a spoken line, both, or `"silent"`, which claims a notification and keeps it quiet.
- Sounds are the macOS sounds or your own files in `~/Library/Application Support/com.jamiewhite.signalladder/Sounds/`, up to 30 seconds long, at a gain from −40 to +12 dB. Every sound is level-matched, so at 0 dB each one peaks at the same level, with a limiter as a safety net.
- A spoken line comes from a template using `{app}`, `{title}` and `{body}`; the default is `{app}: {title}`, and it stops at 240 characters. Each spoken alert has its own voice, rate, pitch and gain, and speech is level-matched per voice. A voice that is not installed is reported, never replaced by another.
- **Test Sound** and **Test Speech** play an alert as it will sound. Test Speech reads a made-up notification, and neither interrupts a real alert.
- A new alert cuts off one still playing, including its speech, so two alarms never sound together.
- The Inspector and menu say what was done without claiming it was heard, such as _Played Glass (+6 dB)_, and note when the Mac's output was muted or at zero volume. The Inspector shows exactly what was said; the menu never does.
- A mute walkthrough in the menu offers **Open Notification Settings for …** and **I've Turned Its Sound Off** for each app a sounding rule reaches, and points to Focus settings. SignalLadder cannot check that an app is muted, and says so.

### Security
- Notification text is held in memory only, and never written to disk or to the logs. The only text on disk is what you put in a rule yourself.
- The mute checklist stores a one-way hash of each app name, never the name, because a name can come from a notification.
- The `signalladder-probe` developer tool prints notification text only when run with `--show-content`.
